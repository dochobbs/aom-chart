Assessment

This is community-acquired pneumonia, likely right lower lobe, with focal crackles/decreased breath sounds, fever, and mild hypoxemia. The SpO₂ of 93% on room air is the main disposition issue. Hypoxemia is associated with greater CAP severity, and some pediatric guidance uses values in the 90–93% range as a reason for a lower threshold for hospital evaluation. 
The Lancet Child & Adolescent Health
+1

Management

Repeat the pulse oximetry after she has rested, ensuring a good waveform/probe position. If the saturation remains around 93%, I would send her to the ED for further evaluation rather than routine outpatient management, particularly given the focal decreased breath sounds. Definite hospitalization is recommended with sustained room-air SpO₂ <90%, but the 90–93% range warrants individualized disposition based on trajectory and exam. 
The Lancet Child & Adolescent Health

Obtain a chest radiograph given the abnormal oxygenation and focal decreased breath sounds. Imaging is specifically recommended when hypoxemia or significant respiratory distress is present and is useful here to assess consolidation and a possible parapneumonic effusion.

 If an effusion is seen and is moderate/large, chest ultrasound is preferred for further characterization.
