This is a presumed first febrile urinary tract infection (UTI), likely upper-tract infection, despite the absence of flank tenderness; toddlers often cannot localize urinary symptoms. The positive nitrite, leukocyte esterase, pyuria, and bacteriuria from a catheterized specimen justify empiric treatment while awaiting culture. A definitive diagnosis requires a single uropathogen at the significant catheterized-culture threshold with pyuria.

DRUG INFORMATION

Start oral cephalexin 25 mg/kg/dose every 8 hours for 7–10 days, assuming normal renal function and acceptable local susceptibility to E. coli. Adjust or narrow therapy when the culture and susceptibilities return. Cephalexin is indicated for susceptible genitourinary pathogens including E. coli, Proteus, and Klebsiella; pediatric administration is divided two to four times daily.
