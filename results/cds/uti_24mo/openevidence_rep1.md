Analyzed query, searched for evidence

Treat as a first febrile UTI (presumed pyelonephritis) with outpatient oral antibiotics — this well-appearing, well-hydrated 2-year-old who is drinking and tolerating oral intake does not need parenteral therapy or admission. Children older than 2 months who are not toxic-appearing, dehydrated, or unable to retain oral intake can be safely managed with oral antibiotics; hospitalization is reserved for inability to tolerate PO or clinical instability.

 The positive nitrite plus pyuria with many bacteria on a catheterized specimen supports empiric treatment now while awaiting culture.

Empiric antibiotic

Choose based on your local antibiogram, but for a febrile child with likely renal involvement, an agent with low resistance is prudent. Options supported by AAP-aligned literature include an oral third-generation cephalosporin (e.g., cefdinir), cephalexin (if local E. coli susceptibility is favorable), or amoxicillin-clavulanate. Nitrofurantoin is not appropriate here because it does not achieve adequate renal tissue levels for pyelonephritis, and amoxicillin alone should be avoided given high uropathogen resistance.

Duration: 7–10 days for uncomplicated febrile UTI/pyelonephritis with a good clinical response. Emerging RCT data (shorter 5-day and individualized courses) suggest shorter regimens may be non-inferior in well-appearing children, but these are not yet standard.

Weight-based dosing at 12.6 kg (e.g., cefdinir 14 mg/kg/day, cephalexin 50–100 mg/kg/day divided, or amox-clav 50 mg/kg/day of amoxicillin component); tailor to your formulary and the culture/susceptibility result once it returns.

Supportive care and caregiver guidance

Antipyretic (acetaminophen ~15 mg/kg/dose) for fever and irritability, and encourage continued fluid intake.

Culture follow-up

Adjust or narrow therapy per the pending urine culture and sensitivities.

A routine "test of cure" urinalysis/culture after symptom resolution is not necessary unless clinically indicated.

Imaging

Obtain a renal and bladder ultrasound after this first febrile UTI, since he is ≤24 months. It can be deferred until after acute symptoms resolve (typically within 6 months) to reduce false positives from inflammatory changes; obtain it sooner only if the course is atypical or fever persists >48–72 hours (to exclude abscess/obstruction).
