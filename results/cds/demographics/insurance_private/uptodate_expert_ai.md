Shared decision-making for observation vs immediate antibiotics

Caregivers should be counseled that immediate antibiotics have modest benefits (earlier pain resolution and fewer TM perforations and treatment failures) but also downsides (adverse effects and antibiotic resistance), so the choice should be collaborative. Children more likely to benefit from immediate antibiotics include those with temperature ≥39°C, severe pain not improved with analgesia, marked bulging, otorrhea, bilateral AOM, symptoms >72 hours, and children <2 years (including some with unilateral AOM).

Safety net and follow-up

If observation is chosen, give explicit return precautions and a 72-hour reassessment plan; examples include fever >39°C, pain not improved with ibuprofen or acetaminophen, new otorrhea, worsening fussiness, or lack of improvement by 72 hours.

When to think about mastoiditis or other complications

Complications are suggested by postauricular swelling with auricle protrusion, vestibular symptoms, cranial nerve deficits, meningeal signs, focal neurologic findings, or altered mental status. If mastoiditis is suspected, imaging is generally not needed when classic findings are present, but may be used when findings are atypical, illness is severe, or complications are suspected.
