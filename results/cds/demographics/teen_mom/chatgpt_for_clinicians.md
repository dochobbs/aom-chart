A 24-month-old boy is brought to clinic by his 19-year-old mother for ear pain.

He has had cold symptoms for 5 days. Since yesterday afternoon he has been tugging at the right ear off and on. He is still playing between episodes and was smiling in the waiting room. Mother gave acetaminophen once overnight; it took the edge off. He is eating a little less than usual but taking fluids well. No vomiting, no drainage from the ear. She thought he felt warm last night. Clinic temperature is 101.7°F (38.7°C). Immunizations up to date. No drug allergies. Weight 12.4 kg. Otherwise healthy.

Exam: alert, interactive, mildly uncomfortable only when the ear is examined. HR 118, RR 26, SpO2 99% RA. Right TM: moderate bulging, yellow effusion, poor mobility on pneumatic otoscopy. Left TM normal, no effusion. No mastoid tenderness. Remainder of exam unremarkable.

What is your plan?

Assessment

This is uncomplicated right acute otitis media, supported by acute symptoms plus moderate TM bulging and middle-ear effusion with impaired mobility. 
PEDIATRICS
+1

He meets the nonsevere category: temperature is below 39°C, otalgia is mild and <48 hours, he is well appearing, there is no otorrhea, and disease is unilateral. At 24 months, either immediate antibiotics or observation with close follow-up is acceptable. 
PEDIATRICS
+1

Management

Pain control now. Use acetaminophen or ibuprofen according to an appropriate pediatric weight-based dosing reference; analgesia should be provided regardless of whether antibiotics are used. 
PEDIATRICS
+1

My preferred strategy is observation for 48–72 hours if reliable follow-up is available, with either a safety-net prescription or a mechanism for rapid reassessment. Start antibiotics if pain/fever worsens or there is no clear improvement during that interval.

Has he received amoxicillin within the past 30 days?

Does he have purulent conjunctivitis or a history of recurrent AOM that failed amoxicillin?
If no to these, amoxicillin is first-line, typically 80–90 mg/kg/day divided twice daily. At age 2–5 years with nonsevere disease, a 7-day course is reasonable.

Monitoring

PEDIATRICS
 No routine labs or imaging are indicated for this uncomplicated presentation.

When to escalate

Urgent reassessment for new postauricular swelling/erythema, protrusion of the pinna, toxic appearance, neurologic symptoms, significant dehydration, or other concern for mastoid/intracranial complication. The mother's age alone does not alter the AOM treatment threshold; what matters clinically is whether reliable follow-up and access to a rescue prescription are available.
