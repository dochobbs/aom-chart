A 24-month-old boy is brought to clinic by his mother for ear pain.

He has had cold symptoms for 5 days. Since yesterday afternoon he has been tugging at the right ear off and on. He is still playing between episodes and was smiling in the waiting room. Mother gave acetaminophen once overnight; it took the edge off. He is eating a little less than usual but taking fluids well. No vomiting, no drainage from the ear. She thought he felt warm last night. Clinic temperature is 101.7°F (38.7°C). Immunizations up to date. No drug allergies. Weight 12.4 kg. Otherwise healthy.

Exam: alert, interactive, mildly uncomfortable only when the ear is examined. HR 118, RR 26, SpO2 99% RA. Right TM: moderate bulging, yellow effusion, poor mobility on pneumatic otoscopy. Left TM normal, no effusion. No mastoid tenderness. Remainder of exam unremarkable.

What is your plan?

Assessment

Acute right otitis media, nonsevere and uncomplicated. The moderate TM bulging plus middle-ear effusion/poor mobility meets diagnostic criteria for AOM. He does not meet severe-AOM criteria: otalgia is mild and <48 hours, temperature is <39°C, and there is no otorrhea or mastoid involvement. 
PEDIATRICS
+1

Management

Offer observation/watchful waiting as the preferred stewardship option, using shared decision-making with his mother. At 24 months, a child with nonsevere unilateral AOM may be observed provided there is a reliable mechanism for reassessment and antibiotic initiation if symptoms worsen or fail to improve within 48–72 hours. A safety-net/delayed prescription is reasonable. 
PEDIATRICS
+2

Treat pain and fever. Continue an appropriate weight-based dose of acetaminophen or ibuprofen as needed; analgesia is recommended whether or not antibiotics are prescribed. 
PEDIATRICS
+1

If the family prefers immediate antibiotics, follow-up cannot be assured, or observation fails: use high-dose amoxicillin 80–90 mg/kg/day PO divided BID as first-line therapy, assuming no amoxicillin exposure within the preceding 30 days, purulent conjunctivitis, or prior recurrent AOM unresponsive to amoxicillin. At age 2 years with nonsevere uncomplicated AOM, a 5–7-day course is appropriate; I would commonly use 7 days in this age group. 
PEDIATRICS
+2

If recent amoxicillin exposure, purulent conjunctivitis, or recurrent AOM previously unresponsive to amoxicillin is present, amoxicillin-clavulanate is preferred instead of amoxicillin alone. 
CDC
+1

Reassess for persistent/worsening otalgia or fever after 48–72 hours. Seek earlier evaluation for postauricular swelling/tenderness, auricular displacement, toxic appearance, severe headache, persistent vomiting, or other concerning deterioration. 
PEDIATRICS
+1

The two chart elements that materially affect the final plan are whether reliable 48–72-hour follow-up is available and, if antibiotics are needed, whether he received amoxicillin in the past 30 days or has a history of recurrent AOM unresponsive to amoxicillin. Counseling and shared decision-making should be conducted through the Spanish interpreter.
