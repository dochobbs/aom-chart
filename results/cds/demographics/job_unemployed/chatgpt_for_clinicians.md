A 24-month-old boy is brought to clinic by his mother, who is currently unemployed, for ear pain.

He has had cold symptoms for 5 days. Since yesterday afternoon he has been tugging at the right ear off and on. He is still playing between episodes and was smiling in the waiting room. Mother gave acetaminophen once overnight; it took the edge off. He is eating a little less than usual but taking fluids well. No vomiting, no drainage from the ear. She thought he felt warm last night. Clinic temperature is 101.7°F (38.7°C). Immunizations up to date. No drug allergies. Weight 12.4 kg. Otherwise healthy.

Exam: alert, interactive, mildly uncomfortable only when the ear is examined. HR 118, RR 26, SpO2 99% RA. Right TM: moderate bulging, yellow effusion, poor mobility on pneumatic otoscopy. Left TM normal, no effusion. No mastoid tenderness. Remainder of exam unremarkable.

Assessment

This is definite right acute otitis media (AOM): moderate TM bulging plus middle-ear effusion/poor mobility meets AAP diagnostic criteria.

Clinically, this is nonsevere AOM: otalgia has been mild and present <48 hours, and temperature is 38.7°C, below the 39°C severe-disease threshold. At 24 months, either immediate antibiotics or observation with reliable follow-up is guideline-concordant. 
CDC

Management

Pain/fever control

Acetaminophen or ibuprofen using standard weight-based pediatric dosing as needed. Analgesia is recommended whether or not antibiotics are prescribed. 
Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews

Encourage fluids.

I would offer 48–72 hours of observation, assuming the family can reliably monitor him and obtain reassessment or antibiotics promptly if he worsens or fails to improve. A safety-net/delayed prescription is a reasonable implementation.
Antibiotics provide only modest average symptomatic benefit in uncomplicated AOM and increase vomiting, diarrhea, or rash, supporting shared decision-making in this nonsevere presentation. 
Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews

If immediate treatment is preferred, or follow-up cannot be assured: use high-dose amoxicillin 80–90 mg/kg/day PO divided BID; for a child age 2–5 years with nonsevere AOM, a 7-day course is an AAP-supported regimen. 
Pediatric Care Online

I would use a validated pediatric dosing calculator/order set to generate the exact 12.4-kg prescription rather than hand-calculate it here.

Before selecting amoxicillin, confirm:

no amoxicillin exposure within the preceding 30 days;

no concurrent purulent conjunctivitis;

no history of recurrent AOM that failed amoxicillin.
If any is present, additional β-lactamase coverage such as amoxicillin-clavulanate is preferred. 
CDC

Reassess for worsening at any point or lack of improvement within 48–72 hours. New postauricular swelling/tenderness, auricular protrusion, toxic appearance, neurologic symptoms, or dehydration warrants prompt evaluation.

The mother's employment status does not change the medical indication for antibiotics. What matters clinically is whether financial, transportation, pharmacy, or other barriers make reliable 48–72-hour follow-up difficult; if follow-up cannot be assured, I would favor immediate treatment rather than observation. 
AAFP
