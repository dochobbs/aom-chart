Assessment

Right acute otitis media, unilateral and nonsevere. Moderate TM bulging plus middle-ear effusion establishes AOM. His otalgia has been mild and <48 hours, temperature is 38.7°C (<39°C), and there is no otorrhea or toxic appearance. 
CDC

Management

For an 18-month-old with unilateral, nonsevere AOM, either immediate antibiotic therapy or observation with close follow-up is guideline-concordant. Observation is appropriate only if the caregiver agrees and there is a reliable mechanism to initiate antibiotics if symptoms worsen or fail to improve within 48–72 hours. 
Pediatric Quality & Safety

My approach:

Treat pain now. Use weight-appropriate acetaminophen or, since he is >6 months, ibuprofen as needed for otalgia/fever. Analgesia is recommended regardless of whether antibiotics are prescribed. 
CDC

Shared decision: observation vs immediate treatment.

Given that he is well appearing with unilateral, nonsevere disease, I would favor a 48–72-hour observation/safety-net strategy if the family can reliably follow up.

Immediate antibiotics are also reasonable; antibiotics provide a modest symptomatic benefit but increase diarrhea, rash, and vomiting. 
Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews

If antibiotics are started: first-line is high-dose amoxicillin 80–90 mg/kg/day PO divided BID for 10 days in a child <2 years, provided there has been no amoxicillin exposure in the preceding 30 days, no purulent conjunctivitis, and no history of recurrent AOM unresponsive to amoxicillin. 
Pediatric Care Online

I would not calculate the 11-kg patient-specific dose without an approved pediatric dosing calculator.

If recent amoxicillin exposure, purulent conjunctivitis, or prior recurrent AOM unresponsive to amoxicillin is present, use an agent with β-lactamase coverage rather than plain amoxicillin.

Reassess for persistent/worsening pain or fever, new otorrhea, or failure to improve within 48–72 hours; mastoid swelling/tenderness, auricular displacement, toxicity, or neurologic symptoms warrant urgent evaluation.

Questions to clarify

Two chart elements determine the final prescription strategy:

Can the family reliably obtain follow-up/start a rescue prescription within 48–72 hours if he worsens or does not improve?

Has he received amoxicillin in the past 30 days, had purulent conjunctivitis, or had recurrent AOM previously unresponsive to amoxicillin?
