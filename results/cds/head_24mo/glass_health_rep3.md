# Glass Health — Minor Head Injury (24 Months) (Rep 3)

This child is intermediate risk by PECARN because of a nonfrontal occipital scalp hematoma and one episode of vomiting, but he has no high-risk findings. With normal mental status, GCS 15, normal neurologic examination, and a low-energy fall, I would favor ED observation rather than immediate CT, provided reliable serial examinations are available.





Plan




Observe with serial neurologic examinations for approximately 4–6 hours from injury, including mental status, behavior, pupils, gait, vomiting, headache/irritability, and development of focal deficits.
No routine laboratory testing or skull radiographs.
No immediate CT if he remains clinically stable; avoid radiation when observation is appropriate. Obtain a noncontrast head CT promptly for worsening mental status, recurrent or worsening vomiting, seizure, persistent severe headache, focal neurologic findings, signs of basilar/depressed skull fracture, or an enlarging/tense scalp swelling. [1][4]
Provide oral fluids when alert and reassess tolerance. Use acetaminophen if needed; avoid sedating medications that could obscure neurologic reassessment.
Reassess the history and injury mechanism privately as appropriate; the current examination does not identify additional injuries or findings suspicious for abuse.




Disposition




Discharge only if he remains GCS 15, neurologically normal, clinically at baseline, without recurrent vomiting or symptom progression, and his father can reliably observe him and return promptly. Give written precautions for repeated vomiting, increasing somnolence or difficulty awakening, confusion or abnormal behavior, seizure, worsening headache, weakness, gait change, unequal pupils, or fluid/blood from the nose or ears. [2]





If symptoms persist or evolve, arrange pediatric follow-up within 24–48 hours; provide concussion-style relative rest and gradual return to usual activity based on symptoms. [3]
