PECARN classification: Low risk. The validated negative predictive value for clinically important traumatic brain injury in low-risk patients under 2 years exceeds 99.9%.

Immediate management

Observation versus neuroimaging: Do not obtain head CT. The risk of radiation-induced malignancy in a 2-year-old (estimated lifetime attributable risk of approximately 1 cancer per 500–1000 head CTs) outweighs the <0.05% probability of clinically important intracranial injury in this low-risk cohort.

Monitoring period: Observe in clinic or recommend home observation for 4–6 hours. The PECARN validation study found that patients who developed deterioration did so within 6 hours in the vast majority of cases.

Parental counseling and return precautions

Provide explicit written instructions. Parents should return immediately or call emergency services if any of the following occur:

Repeated vomiting (≥2 additional episodes)
Altered mental status: unusual sleepiness, difficulty to wake, or not recognizing parents
Seizure
Worsening headache or persistent crying that cannot be comforted
Weakness, difficulty walking, or asymmetry of movements
Clear fluid from nose or ears (CSF leak)

Reassessment: Telephone follow-up within 24 hours or clinic follow-up in 24–48 hours to confirm continued normal behavior and absence of delayed symptoms.
