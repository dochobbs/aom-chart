NEJM Review

According to an invited review in the New England Journal of Medicine, this child meets the defining criterion for acute otitis media (AOM) — moderate bulging of the tympanic membrane with a yellow effusion and impaired mobility on pneumatic otoscopy — which is the single most important finding distinguishing AOM from otitis media with effusion. 
NEJM
[1]
 High-dose amoxicillin (80–90 mg/kg/day divided into two doses) is the recommended first-line antibiotic. 
NEJM
[1]
 Amoxicillin–clavulanate is reserved for children in whom H. influenzae is likely to predominate — those who received antibiotics within the prior 30 days, have conjunctivitis–otitis syndrome, or have spontaneous tympanic membrane rupture — none of which apply here. 
NEJM
[1]
 Oral cephalosporins are generally discouraged because they are substantially less effective than amoxicillin at eradicating penicillin-nonsusceptible S. pneumoniae. 
NEJM
[1]

The review notes that because AOM symptoms often abate without antibiotics and suppurative complications are rare, expectant observation is a reasonable option in nonsevere cases, though severity definitions are not well standardized and trials have not definitively linked severity measures to antibiotic efficacy. 
NEJM
[1]
 Antibiotic-associated harms are relevant to shared decision-making: in a meta-analysis of 82 studies, diarrhea occurred in roughly 1 in 8 children treated with amoxicillin (and 1 in 5 with amoxicillin–clavulanate), and early-life antibiotic exposure has been associated with later allergy, asthma, and obesity. 
NEJM
[1]
 On duration, a 10-day course produced less treatment failure and less rescue-antibiotic use than a 5-day course, which is particularly relevant in a child under 2 years.

Recommended plan for this 24-month-old (12.4 kg, immunizations current, no allergies). This is an unambiguous unilateral AOM with a bulging TM and fever of 38.7°C — a nonsevere presentation, so both immediate antibiotics and observation with close follow-up are defensible, but at this age treatment is generally favored and the child is <2 years, which supports a full 10-day course if antibiotics are given.
