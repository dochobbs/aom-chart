Assessing AOM management in a 24-month-old with unilateral non-severe symptoms

AAP guidelines recommend observation as an option for children 24 months and older with unilateral acute otitis media when diagnosis is certain and symptoms are not severe, particularly with fever below 39°C and no toxic appearance. The validated clinical decision tool to guide this choice is the AAP 2023 AOM management algorithm, which requires input of age, ear involvement (unilateral/bilateral), symptom severity (fever, pain), and diagnostic certainty.

Assessment

Definite acute otitis media (right ear). Diagnostic certainty is high based on moderate TM bulging with effusion and poor pneumatic mobility. The presentation lacks severe features: he is nontoxic and interactive; ear pain has been present less than 48 hours; maximum temperature is <39°C (102.2°F); and the mother is medically sophisticated with reliable access to follow-up

Management Options

Present two pathways and decide with the mother:

Option 1: Observation with close follow-up
Reasonable given age ≥24 months, unilateral disease, mild symptoms, and reliable caregiver. If chosen, provide a safety-net antibiotic prescription or ensure same-day access to care; begin antibiotics if no improvement within 48–72 hours or if worsening occurs 
Pediatrics
.

Option 2: Immediate antibiotics
Also acceptable. Benefits include modestly faster symptom resolution and reduced risk of TM perforation or contralateral otitis; drawbacks include adverse effects (vomiting, diarrhea, rash) in approximately 1 in 14 children treated

Symptomatic Care

Analgesia: Continue acetaminophen or ibuprofen for ear pain and fever 
Am Fam Phys
. Weight-based ibuprofen dosing is 10 mg/kg every 6–8 hours as needed (≈120 mg for this child); acetaminophen 15 mg/kg every 4–6 hours as needed (≈185 mg).

Follow-up and Red Flags

Follow-up: Reassess within 48–72 hours if observing; otherwise, return if not improving by 48–72 hours after starting antibiotics or sooner if worsening.

Red flags requiring urgent reevaluation:

Worsening ear pain or new ear drainage
Temperature ≥39°C persisting >48 hours
Toxic appearance, lethargy, or poor oral intake
