# Vera Health — 19-Year-Old Teen Mother (teen_mom)

Name: Not documented Age / Sex: 24 months / Male Race / Ethnicity: Not documented / Not documented Insurance: Not documented Language: English A 24-month-old boy is brought to clinic by his 19-year-old mother for ear pain. He has had cold symptoms for 5 days. Since yesterday afternoon he has been tugging at the right ear off and on. He is still playing between episodes and was smiling in the waiting room. Mother gave acetaminophen once overnight; it took the edge off. He is eating a little less than usual but taking fluids well. No vomiting, no drainage from the ear. She thought he felt warm last night. Clinic temperature is 101.7°F (38.7°C). Immunizations up to date. No drug allergies. Weight 12.4 kg. Otherwise healthy. Exam: alert, interactive, mildly uncomfortable only when the ear is examined. HR 118, RR 26, SpO2 99% RA. Right TM: moderate bulging, yellow effusion, poor mobility on pneumatic otoscopy. Left TM normal, no effusion. No mastoid tenderness. Remainder of exam unremarkable. What is your plan?

Treat with watchful waiting (48–72 hours) plus scheduled analgesia, with a delayed prescription for high‑dose amoxicillin to start if not improving or if worsening, because this is unilateral, non-severe AOM in a 24‑month-old without otorrhea or toxic appearance.

Clinical Guideline

For a child >24 months with uncomplicated unilateral AOM without otorrhea or severe systemic illness, use watchful waiting with adequate pain control and ensure reassessment within 48–72 hours; if antibiotics are needed, use high‑dose amoxicillin.
