Treat with high-dose amoxicillin plus scheduled analgesia — this child meets firm diagnostic criteria for acute otitis media (moderate TM bulging, yellow effusion, reduced mobility on pneumatic otoscopy), and at 24 months with a documented fever of 38.7°C, immediate antibiotics are favored over watchful waiting.

Antibiotic choice and dose

High-dose amoxicillin 80–90 mg/kg/day divided BID is first-line, as there is no antibiotic in the prior 30 days, no purulent conjunctivitis, and no penicillin allergy. At 12.4 kg this is roughly 500 mg PO BID (~990–1,116 mg/day).

Duration: 10 days, because a full course reduces treatment failure and rescue-antibiotic use versus 5 days, and 10 days is standard for children younger than 2 years.

Reserve amoxicillin-clavulanate for the H. influenzae–predominant scenarios (recent antibiotics, conjunctivitis-otitis syndrome, or TM rupture) — none apply here. Avoid oral cephalosporins and macrolides as first-line; macrolides are inadequate due to pneumococcal resistance.

Pain and fever control

Analgesia is recommended for all AOM and is the mainstay of symptom relief regardless of the antibiotic decision. Continue weight-based acetaminophen and/or ibuprofen (the child is >6 months, well-hydrated, and taking fluids) on a scheduled basis for the first 24–48 hours rather than as needed. 
AFP
[3]

Caregiver counseling and follow-up

Advise the young mother on the full 10-day course, expected improvement within 48–72 hours, and to complete the medication even after symptoms resolve.

Return/precaution instructions — persistent or worsening fever, increasing irritability or lethargy, new ear drainage (otorrhea), or postauricular swelling/tenderness (mastoiditis) — warrant reassessment for treatment failure or complication. 
Pediatrics in Review
[5]

Reinforce continued oral hydration; he is currently well-hydrated (playful, interactive, normal vitals, eating slightly less but drinking well), so no acute concern.

No routine follow-up visit is required if he improves; a middle-ear effusion may persist for weeks after resolution of infection and does not itself require further antibiotics.
