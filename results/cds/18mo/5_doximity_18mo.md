Assessment: Right acute otitis media (AOM), confirmed by acute ear symptoms with moderate TM bulging, effusion, and reduced mobility. He is otherwise well appearing and has nonsevere unilateral AOM: mild otalgia for <48 hours, temperature <39°C, no otorrhea, and no mastoid findings.

Plan

Shared decision-making: observation with reliable follow-up is appropriate. At 18 months with unilateral, nonsevere AOM, either immediate antibiotics or watchful waiting is guideline-concordant. Given his mild symptoms and reassuring appearance, I would offer observation for 48–72 hours with a safety-net/delayed prescription. 
Pediatrics

Analgesia/antipyresis

Acetaminophen 15 mg/kg/dose PO every 4–6 hours as needed (for 11 kg: 165 mg/dose; do not exceed 75 mg/kg/day).
Ibuprofen 10 mg/kg/dose PO every 6–8 hours as needed if age >6 months and adequately hydrated (for 11 kg: 110 mg/dose; maximum 40 mg/kg/day).
Encourage fluids; appetite may be reduced temporarily. Avoid OTC cough/cold medications.

Delayed antibiotic prescription: Start if ear pain/fever worsens at any point or if there is no meaningful improvement by 48–72 hours.

Amoxicillin 400 mg/5 mL suspension: 6 mL PO twice daily for 10 days (approximately 87 mg/kg/day).
No reported penicillin allergy, recent amoxicillin exposure, purulent conjunctivitis, or recurrent AOM unresponsive to amoxicillin to warrant broader initial coverage. High-dose amoxicillin is appropriate first-line therapy if antibiotics are selected.

Return/urgent evaluation precautions: persistent or escalating fever, worsening or persistent otalgia after 48–72 hours, poor oral intake/decreased urine output, new otorrhea, postauricular erythema/swelling/tenderness, ear displacement, lethargy, or other clinical deterioration.

Follow-up: Parent to contact clinic in 48–72 hours if not improving or sooner for concerns. Routine recheck is not needed if symptoms fully resolve; reassess if recurrent episodes or persistent hearing/speech concerns.
