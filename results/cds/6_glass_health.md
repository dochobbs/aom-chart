This is acute unilateral right otitis media with definite effusion and moderate TM bulging. Because he is exactly 24 months, has unilateral disease, is well appearing, has only mild–moderate pain, and temperature is below 39°C, either immediate antibiotics or 48-hour observation is reasonable; I would offer observation with a safety-net prescription if reliable follow-up is assured. [1]





Analgesia: acetaminophen 15 mg/kg PO every 4–6 hours as needed (about 185 mg/dose; follow product concentration limits), or ibuprofen 10 mg/kg PO every 6–8 hours as needed if adequately hydrated (about 125 mg/dose). Do not routinely alternate them. [2]

Observation: reassess or start the backup antibiotic if pain or fever is not clearly improving within 48 hours, or sooner if worsening.
Backup antibiotic: amoxicillin 90 mg/kg/day PO divided twice daily. For 12.4 kg: approximately 560 mg PO twice daily. Because he is 24 months with nonsevere disease, use 7 days; use 10 days if symptoms become severe or the clinician considers him effectively under 2 years. [3][4]

Escalation: urgent evaluation for toxic appearance, persistent severe pain, postauricular swelling/tenderness, neck stiffness, dehydration, new facial weakness, or clinical deterioration.
Follow-up: no routine ear recheck is required if symptoms resolve. Persistent effusion alone after recovery is common and does not warrant additional antibiotics. [3]
