Use initial observation (with either no prescription or a delayed prescription) and give explicit return precautions and a clear plan to start antibiotics or be re-evaluated if symptoms worsen or fail to improve within 72 hours. Examples of “worsening” that can be operationalized for caregivers include fever >39°C, pain not improved with ibuprofen or acetaminophen, new ear drainage, worsening fussiness, or lack of improvement by 72 hours.

If antibiotics are started now or after failed observation

For most children, the preferred initial antibiotic is high-dose amoxicillin. If the child received a beta-lactam antibiotic in the prior 30 days or has recurrent AOM unresponsive to amoxicillin, use high-dose amoxicillin-clavulanate instead.

Follow-up

If symptoms worsen or fail to improve after 72 hours of observation, start antibiotics. If symptoms worsen or fail to improve after 72 hours of antibiotic therapy, switch to a second-line antimicrobial agent and reassess for complications or an alternate diagnosis. Routine follow-up is not required after symptom resolution, but children with recurrent infections, chronic hearing loss, or language or learning problems should be reexamined 8 to 12 weeks after diagnosis to ensure effusion resolution.
