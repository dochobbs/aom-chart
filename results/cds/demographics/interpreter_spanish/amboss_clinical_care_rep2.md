Immediate management
Provide oral analgesia now. Acetaminophen or ibuprofen are considered mainstays for mild-to-moderate AOM pain, and pain treatment should be provided whether or not antibiotics are prescribed. 
2
Encourage oral fluids and usual activity as tolerated. The available sources do not provide a patient-specific acetaminophen or ibuprofen dose, so dosing should follow the product label or local pediatric dosing protocol.
Explain that antibiotics generally do not relieve pain during the first 24 hours, so analgesia remains important even if antibiotic treatment is started. 
2
Antibiotic decision

Because he is 24 months old, has unilateral disease, mild symptoms for less than 48 hours, temperature below 39°C, no otorrhea, and a well appearance, either of the following is reasonable:

Observation with a safety-net prescription, provided the family can reliably communicate and obtain treatment if needed. Antibiotics should be started if symptoms worsen at any time or fail to improve within 48–72 hours. 
2
Immediate antibiotic treatment after shared decision-making with the mother. Immediate therapy may modestly accelerate symptom and otoscopic resolution but exposes the child to adverse effects such as diarrhea and rash. 
2

If choosing observation, provide a written or electronic rescue prescription and clear instructions not to start it unless symptoms worsen or fail to improve within 48–72 hours. 
2

If choosing immediate treatment or delayed treatment that becomes necessary:

Amoxicillin 80–90 mg/kg/day orally, divided twice daily, for 5–7 days.

At 12.4 kg, this corresponds to approximately 992–1,116 mg/day, or 496–558 mg per dose twice daily.
Use amoxicillin/clavulanate instead of amoxicillin if there was amoxicillin exposure during the previous 30 days, concurrent purulent conjunctivitis, or recurrent AOM previously unresponsive to amoxicillin. None of those factors is documented here.

Follow-up and safety net
Reassess within 48–72 hours if symptoms worsen or do not improve. If initial amoxicillin fails, amoxicillin/clavulanate is the recommended next oral therapy. 
1
2
Seek urgent evaluation for postauricular swelling or tenderness, toxic appearance, persistent vomiting, severe or worsening pain, new otorrhea, vertigo, hearing loss, facial weakness, severe headache, or neurologic symptoms, because these findings raise concern for suppurative or intracranial complications.
