Evaluating febrile seizure workup in infancy

The presence of a prolonged seizure and age under 12 months increases the risk of bacterial meningitis, warranting lumbar puncture in the absence of clear viral etiology. Guidelines recommend LP for all infants under 12 months with first febrile seizure, especially with fever above 38.5°C and no clear source. Search for the validated clinical decision rule for lumbar puncture in febrile infants and the required patient inputs including age, temperature, seizure duration, and clinical signs.

Assessing anticonvulsant dosing in infantile febrile seizures

The 2011 AAP guideline recommends against routine use of antipyretics for seizure prevention, and no anticonvulsant regimen has shown benefit in reducing recurrence beyond placebo in this age group. The evidence does not support prophylactic medication for first-time febrile seizures in otherwise healthy infants, and dosing algorithms remain undefined outside of acute seizure control. Assessment

Most likely diagnosis: complex febrile seizure secondary to viral illness.

Key features supporting this: age-appropriate (6–60 months), fever >38°C, no focal findings, normal mental status after post-ictal period, viral prodrome.

Must exclude: bacterial meningitis (1 in 4 children with meningitis present with seizure), encephalitis, electrolyte disturbance, or other provoked seizure etiologies.

Immediate actions

Observation and monitoring: Observe for a minimum of 4–6 hours in an acute care setting (ED or observation unit) to monitor for seizure recurrence, which occurs in approximately 4–5% of children with first complex febrile seizure during the acute period

Parent counseling

Risk of recurrence: Approximately 30–40% risk of another febrile seizure with future fevers; risk factors include age <18 months, fever duration <1 hour before seizure, family history of febrile seizures, and temperature <40°C (104°F)

Follow-up

Primary care follow-up: Within 24–48 hours.

Pediatric neurology referral: Consider if any atypical features emerge, if there are recurrent febrile seizures, or if developmental concerns arise.
