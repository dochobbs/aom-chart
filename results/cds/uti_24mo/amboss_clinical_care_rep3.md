This presentation is most consistent with a first febrile UTI, clinically concerning for upper-tract involvement, despite the absence of flank pain or other localizing symptoms. Fever may be the only sign of UTI in young children, and the urinalysis shows pyuria and bacteriuria.

Immediate management

Start empiric oral antibiotic therapy today while awaiting the urine culture and susceptibility results. Positive nitrites, leukocyte esterase, urine white blood cells, and bacteria support empiric treatment. 
1

Cefixime is a reasonable empiric choice, provided it is consistent with local resistance patterns and the child has no relevant contraindication. The pediatric dose is 8 mg/kg/day orally once daily or divided every 12 hours.

Disposition and escalation

Outpatient treatment is reasonable because he is alert, hemodynamically stable, drinking, and without vomiting, dehydration, or severe illness. Admission and parenteral therapy should be considered if he develops severe illness, cannot tolerate oral fluids or antibiotics, requires IV therapy, has significant urinary tract anomalies, or has barriers to reliable follow-up. 
1

If fever or clinical symptoms persist beyond 48–72 hours after starting effective therapy, obtain renal and bladder ultrasound during the acute illness to evaluate for obstruction or renal/perinephric abscess, review the culture, and consider specialist consultation. 
1

Provide supportive care with fluids and an age- and weight-appropriate antipyretic for discomfort; antipyretics improve comfort but do not treat the infection. 
8

Imaging and subsequent prevention
Arrange a renal and bladder ultrasound after resolution of this first febrile UTI because he is 24 months old. For an uncomplicated response, imaging is typically obtained after the acute infection, often within approximately 6 weeks. 
1
Do not obtain a routine VCUG now if the ultrasound is normal and the course is typical. VCUG is indicated if the ultrasound shows hydronephrosis, scarring, or findings suggesting high-grade vesicoureteral reflux or obstruction, or if he develops another febrile UTI. 
1
3
A DMSA scan is not routinely indicated after a first uncomplicated febrile UTI. 
3
Counsel the family to seek prompt evaluation, ideally within 48 hours, for future unexplained fevers because early recognition and treatment of recurrent febrile UTIs is important. 
3
Assess for constipation or bladder-bowel dysfunction if symptoms recur, because these conditions increase UTI risk.
