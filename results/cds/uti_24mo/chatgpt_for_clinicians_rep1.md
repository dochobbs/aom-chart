A 24-month-old boy is brought to clinic by his mother for fever.

He has been fussy and warm for 2 days. Mother has not measured a temperature at home. He is drinking well but eating less. One loose stool yesterday. No vomiting, no cough, no runny nose, no rash. Wet diapers as usual. Clinic temperature is 38.4°C (101.1°F). Immunizations up to date. No drug allergies. Weight 12.6 kg. Otherwise healthy.

Exam: alert, fussy but consolable, drinking from a cup in the room. HR 128, RR 28, SpO2 99% RA. TMs normal. Throat clear. Lungs clear. Abdomen soft, non-tender, no masses. No CVA tenderness. Genital exam unremarkable. No rash. Remainder of exam unremarkable.

Catheterized urinalysis: leukocyte esterase 2+, nitrite positive, 30 WBC/hpf, many bacteria. Urine culture sent.

What is your plan?

Assessment

This is a presumed febrile UTI / acute pyelonephritis in a well-appearing 24-month-old. The catheterized UA is strongly supportive (pyuria, nitrite+, bacteriuria), and the culture was appropriately obtained before antibiotics. Definitive microbiologic diagnosis depends on growth of a uropathogen from the catheterized culture. 
ncbi.nlm.nih.gov

Management

Treat empirically now; do not wait for the culture. Febrile pediatric UTI should receive prompt antimicrobial therapy, then be narrowed or changed when organism and susceptibilities return. 
JAMA Pediatrics
+1

He is drinking, nontoxic, and has no vomiting, so oral therapy is appropriate; oral and parenteral therapy have similar efficacy in uncomplicated febrile UTI when the child can tolerate oral medication. 
PEDIATRICS

A reasonable outpatient choice is cefixime 8 mg/kg/day PO once daily, assuming this fits the local antibiogram. Other reasonable choices include cephalexin, amoxicillin-clavulanate, or another oral cephalosporin according to local E. coli susceptibility.

I would use a 7–10 day course for this febrile UTI rather than a short cystitis regimen. Current evidence continues to favor ≥7 days for febrile pediatric UTI. 
PEDIATRICS
+1

Do not use nitrofurantoin for a febrile UTI/possible pyelonephritis because tissue/serum levels are inadequate for upper-tract infection.

Acetaminophen/ibuprofen as needed for fever or discomfort; encourage fluids.

Monitoring

Review the urine culture and susceptibilities within ~48 hours and tailor therapy. If the culture does not confirm a clinically significant uropathogen, reassess the diagnosis rather than automatically completing antibiotics.

I would expect clear clinical improvement within about 48 hours. Persistent fever, worsening appearance, vomiting/inability to take medication, dehydration, or other systemic deterioration warrants reassessment for resistant organism, obstruction/abscess, bacteremia, and need for parenteral therapy or admission. Failure to respond within 48 hours is considered an atypical course. 
ncbi.nlm.nih.gov

Workup

For a first febrile UTI at this age, I would obtain a renal/bladder ultrasound (RBUS) after the acute illness/once the diagnosis is confirmed. Contemporary EAU/ESPU guidance generally recommends RBUS after an initial febrile UTI, and US is also supported by US pediatric imaging guidance. 
Journal of Pediatric Urology
+1

I would not obtain a routine VCUG after this first uncomplicated episode. Consider VCUG if the RBUS is abnormal, the organism is atypical/non-E. coli, the course is complicated, or he subsequently develops another febrile UTI. 
PEDIATRICS
+1

No routine test-of-cure culture is needed if he becomes asymptomatic, and no prophylactic antibiotic is indicated after an uncomplicated first UTI. 
ncbi.nlm.nih.gov

Disposition: outpatient treatment is appropriate based on the information given, with culture follow-up and reassessment if he is not clearly improving within 48 hours.
