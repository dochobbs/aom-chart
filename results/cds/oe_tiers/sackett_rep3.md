Why observation is legitimate at 24 months

The AAP places children 24 months and older without severe symptoms (unilateral or bilateral) squarely in the observation-eligible group; the same applies to 6–23-month-olds with non-severe unilateral disease, so this child qualifies under either reading of his exact age. 
Red Book
[1]

Most AOM in vaccinated, otherwise-healthy children in high-income settings resolves spontaneously, and antibiotics provide only modest benefit. The Cochrane review found antibiotics do not reduce pain at 24 hours and yield an NNT of ~7 for pain reduction at days 10–12, while roughly 1 in 14 treated children experiences an adverse event (vomiting, diarrhea, or rash). Antibiotic benefit is most pronounced in children under 2 with bilateral disease or with otorrhea — neither present here.

Pain control is the priority in either arm

Analgesia is the mainstay of AOM management regardless of the antibiotic decision. Weight-based acetaminophen (~15 mg/kg ≈ 185 mg q4–6h) and/or ibuprofen (~10 mg/kg ≈ 124 mg q6–8h) — both effective for otalgia; he is >6 months so ibuprofen is appropriate given good fluid intake.

Return precautions / red flags for the mother

Return promptly for fever or pain persisting/worsening beyond 48–72 hours, new ear drainage, postauricular swelling/redness or protruding ear (mastoiditis), neck stiffness, lethargy, or poor fluid intake. No routine follow-up otoscopy is needed if he recovers, though an effusion may persist for weeks after symptoms resolve.
