Analyzed query, searched for evidence

Outpatient management with high-dose oral amoxicillin is appropriate for this previously healthy, fully immunized 5-year-old with focal, lobar-pattern community-acquired pneumonia (CAP). She is well-appearing, talking in full sentences, hydrating well, with no retractions or nasal flaring and capillary refill under 2 seconds — none of the IDSA criteria mandating hospitalization are met, with the one caveat that her SpO2 of 93% sits just at the borderline and warrants a recheck.

Assessment of severity and disposition

Reassess oxygenation before deciding. IDSA lists SpO2 persistently below 90–92% on room air as an indication for admission. At 93% she is marginal — recheck the saturation (ensure a good waveform/warm extremity), and observe her in clinic. Sustained SpO2 ≥92% with good work of breathing supports discharge home; a persistent value of 90–92% or any decline should prompt referral for admission.

Empiric antibiotic — first-line is high-dose oral amoxicillin for S. pneumoniae coverage.

Dose: 90 mg/kg/day. At 18.5 kg that is ~1,665 mg/day, e.g., ~830 mg PO twice daily (or 45 mg/kg/day divided three times daily).

Duration: a short 5-day course is supported by the SAFER RCT and meta-analytic data showing non-inferiority to 10 days in outpatient pediatric CAP; outpatient therapy should not exceed 7 days.

Supportive care and counseling

Antipyretics/analgesia (continue ibuprofen and/or acetaminophen), encourage fluids, and continue monitoring oral intake and breathing.

[1]

Safety-netting: return immediately for fast/labored breathing, retractions, bluish color, poor drinking or reduced urine output, persistent high fever, or lethargy.

Follow-up in 48–72 hours. Children not improving by then need reassessment; failure to improve is also the point at which chest radiography becomes useful.
