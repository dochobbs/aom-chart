Provide supportive care:

Encourage fluids and monitor urine output.

Arrange clinical reassessment within 48–72 hours, or sooner if symptoms worsen. Lack of improvement by 48–72 hours should prompt review of the culture, assessment for resistant organisms or an incorrect diagnosis, and renal/bladder ultrasound to evaluate for obstruction or renal abscess. 
1

Imaging plan

Because this is a first febrile UTI in a child younger than 6 years, obtain a renal and bladder ultrasound after the acute illness has resolved, typically within approximately 6 weeks if he improves normally. 
1

Do not obtain a VCUG routinely after the first febrile UTI. Perform VCUG if the ultrasound shows hydronephrosis, scarring, or findings suggesting high-grade vesicoureteral reflux or obstruction, or if he develops another febrile UTI or has an atypical course. 
2

Escalation criteria

Send him for emergency evaluation and consider hospitalization with parenteral antibiotics if he develops toxic appearance, hemodynamic instability, dehydration, persistent vomiting, inability to take oral medication, significant renal abnormality, or unreliable follow-up.
