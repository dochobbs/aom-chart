A 24-month-old boy is brought to clinic by his mother for ear pain.

He has had cold symptoms for 5 days. Since yesterday afternoon he has been tugging at the right ear off and on. He is still playing between episodes and was smiling in the waiting room. Mother gave acetaminophen once overnight; it took the edge off. He is eating a little less than usual but taking fluids well. No vomiting, no drainage from the ear. She thought he felt warm last night. Clinic temperature is 101.7°F (38.7°C). Immunizations up to date. No drug allergies. Weight 12.4 kg. Otherwise healthy.

Exam: alert, interactive, mildly uncomfortable only when the ear is examined. HR 118, RR 26, SpO2 99% RA. Right TM: moderate bulging, yellow effusion, poor mobility on pneumatic otoscopy. Left TM normal, no effusion. No mastoid tenderness. Remainder of exam unremarkable.

Assessment

Uncomplicated right acute otitis media (AOM), nonsevere. Moderate TM bulging plus middle-ear effusion meets diagnostic criteria. His otalgia is mild and <48 hours, temperature is 38.7°C (<39°C), there is no otorrhea, and he is well appearing. 
CDC

Management

I would offer observation/watchful waiting rather than immediate antibiotics, using shared decision-making with the mother. At 24 months, unilateral nonsevere AOM can be observed provided reliable follow-up is available. Reassess/start antibiotics if symptoms worsen or fail to improve within 48–72 hours. 
AAP
+1

This is a particularly reasonable case for observation: unilateral disease, no otorrhea, <39°C fever, mild short-duration pain, drinking adequately, and well appearance. Antibiotics provide only modest average symptomatic benefit in uncomplicated AOM and increase diarrhea/rash/vomiting. 
Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews
+2

Pain/fever control is important regardless of antibiotic choice. Use oral acetaminophen or ibuprofen according to appropriate pediatric weight-based dosing; I would not routinely alternate the two because this increases dosing-error risk without established additional benefit. 
Pediatrics in Review

Safety-net prescription is reasonable if follow-up logistics make it useful: instruct the parent to start it only if pain/fever is worsening or there is no clear improvement over the next 48–72 hours. 
PEDIATRICS

If antibiotics are started: amoxicillin is first line if he has not received amoxicillin in the preceding 30 days and does not have purulent conjunctivitis or prior recurrent AOM unresponsive to amoxicillin. The guideline regimen is amoxicillin 80–90 mg/kg/day PO divided BID; for uncomplicated AOM in children age 2–5 years, a 7-day course is guideline-concordant. 
AAP
+1

I would not calculate the 12.4-kg patient-specific mg or mL dose here without an approved clinical dose calculator.

If he had amoxicillin within 30 days, purulent conjunctivitis, or recurrent AOM previously unresponsive to amoxicillin, amoxicillin-clavulanate rather than plain amoxicillin is recommended. 
CDC

Monitoring

Return/reassess sooner for rapidly worsening pain, new otorrhea, toxic appearance, dehydration, postauricular swelling/erythema or auricular protrusion. Otherwise reassess if not clearly improving within 48–72 hours. Persistent asymptomatic middle-ear effusion afterward does not by itself indicate additional antibiotics. 
AAFP

My default plan for this child: analgesia + 48–72-hour observation/safety-net strategy, rather than immediate antibiotics, assuming reliable follow-up.
