Clinical risk stratification

For children under 2 years with minor head trauma, PECARN identifies three risk categories 
Lancet
 +1
:

Risk category	Criteria	ciTBI risk	Recommended action
High	GCS < 15, altered mental status, palpable skull fracture	~4.4%	CT indicated
Intermediate	Non-frontal scalp hematoma, LOC > 5 seconds, not acting normally per parent, severe mechanism*	~0.9%	Observation vs. CT (shared decision-making)
Very low	None of above	< 0.02%	No CT needed

*Severe mechanism = fall > 3 feet (91 cm), MVA with ejection/rollover/fatality, pedestrian/bicyclist struck, or struck by high-impact object

Immediate management

Observation period: 4 to 6 hours of in-person clinical monitoring

 +2
. During this interval:

Perform serial neurological examinations every 1 to 2 hours
Assess for changes in mental status, gait, or neurological function
Monitor for repeated vomiting, worsening headache, or behavioral changes
Observe for deterioration in consciousness or new focal findings

Disposition and follow-up

If the child remains asymptomatic with stable examinations throughout the observation period:

Discharge home with responsible caregiver
Provide explicit return precautions (worsening headache, repeated vomiting, unusual sleepiness, confusion, seizure, weakness, or behavior changes)
Ensure reliable follow-up within 24 hours (phone call or re-evaluation)
Activity restriction: no sports, rough play, or activities risking re-injury until symptom-free for 24 hours

Red flags requiring immediate reassessment:

Repeated vomiting (≥ 2–3 episodes)
Altered mental status or excessive sleepiness
Severe or worsening headache
Seizure activity
Weakness, gait disturbance, or other neurological deficit
Behavior change or irritability not attributable to fatigue
What to avoid
Do not discharge without ensuring reliable observation capacity and return access to care
Do not order CT solely for parental anxiety without informed discussion of radiation risks
Do not dismiss the vomiting episode without acknowledging it as a symptom requiring monitoring, though in PECARN < 2 years it is not a strong independent predictor 
Acad EM
Do not overlook non-accidental trauma; the mechanism (fall from couch while parent in another room) is plausible, but ensure injury pattern is consistent with history and developmental capability
