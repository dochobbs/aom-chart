Two reasonable options, both guideline-concordant, via shared decision-making:

Immediate high-dose amoxicillin. At 24 months with a clearly certain diagnosis, initiating antibiotics is well supported. High-dose amoxicillin 80–90 mg/kg/day divided into two doses is first-line. For this 12.4 kg boy that is roughly 550 mg PO BID (~89 mg/kg/day). Duration: a 10-day course is recommended for children <2 years and for a diagnosis meeting full criteria—10 days produced less treatment failure and less rescue-antibiotic use than 5 days.

Watchful waiting with a safety-net (delayed) prescription. Because this is unilateral, non-severe disease and the child looks well, observation for 48–72 hours with analgesia and a rescue prescription to fill if he fails to improve or worsens is an accepted alternative in children ≥6 months with non-severe unilateral AOM, provided reliable follow-up and caregiver buy-in. Most such episodes resolve spontaneously and only a small minority ultimately need the antibiotic. Antibiotics confer only modest symptomatic benefit and carry a meaningful rate of adverse effects—diarrhea in ~1 in 8 on amoxicillin.
