This child is not low risk by PECARN (nonfrontal scalp hematoma and vomiting), so the preferred plan is ED observation with serial exams for 4 to 6 hours post-injury, reserving head CT for clinical worsening or failure to improve.
+3

ED observation and reassessment

Observation should be “close observation” for 4 to 6 hours after injury, with neuroimaging obtained if symptoms do not improve or if worsening/new symptoms develop.
+1

Factors that can push toward immediate CT rather than observation include multiple findings, worsening or severe symptoms, persistent vomiting, clinical course, and shared decision-making.

Disposition if observation is reassuring

If symptoms improve during the observation period and discharge criteria are met, discharge home is suggested rather than hospital admission.
+1

Discharge criteria include: no suspicion of inflicted injury; normal neurologic exam and easily aroused; at baseline function; tolerating oral intake; no extracranial injuries requiring admission; and capable caretakers who can reliably observe and return if needed.

Discharge instructions

Provide explicit instructions for monitoring and return precautions; it is not necessary to awaken most children, but some children discharged without neuroimaging after concerning mechanism or prolonged symptoms may be awakened every 4 or more hours.

Immediate medical attention is required for inability to awaken, persistent/worsening headache, continued vomiting or vomiting that begins/continues 4 to 6 hours after injury, change in mental status/behavior, unsteady gait/incoordination, or seizure.

Arrange follow-up within 24 hours (at least by telephone).

Helpful decision support: PECARN rules: Findings associated with very low risk of significant traumatic brain injury in children and Approach to neuroimaging for children with minor blunt head trauma.
