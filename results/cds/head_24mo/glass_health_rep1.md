# Glass Health — Minor Head Injury (24 Months) (Rep 1)

This child is intermediate risk for ciTBI because of a nonfrontal occipital scalp hematoma and one episode of vomiting, but his normal mental status, GCS 15, normal neurologic examination, and low-energy fall support structured observation rather than immediate CT. Obtain a careful history of loss of consciousness, abnormal behavior, severe mechanism, worsening symptoms, and repeat vomiting; verify caregiver reliability and injury consistency. <cite s="3b34ab96-e0ad-508d-9878-7b6af1ec7415"/>

Plan

Observe in clinic/ED-capable setting: Serial mental-status, neurologic, pupillary, gait, vomiting, headache/behavior, and vital-sign assessments for approximately 4–6 hours after injury.
Avoid routine skull radiographs.
Escalate to noncontrast head CT and emergency transfer for declining consciousness, persistent/worsening headache, repeated vomiting, seizure, focal neurologic deficit, abnormal behavior, palpable skull depression, signs of basilar skull fracture, or other clinical deterioration. <cite s="6bb00f4e-1df5-51e0-8c13-a806f02a5663"/>

If he remains at baseline, tolerates oral fluids, has no further vomiting, and the examination remains normal, discharge with his father after shared decision-making. Provide written and verbal precautions: return immediately for repeated/prolonged vomiting, worsening headache, confusion or unusual irritability, excessive sleepiness or difficulty waking, seizure, weakness, gait change, unequal pupils, or behavioral deterioration. A responsible adult should supervise him for the first 24 hours. <cite s="00d9c03a-60bb-5bfd-9984-21e7bd5361e6"/>

Treat as possible concussion if symptoms persist or emerge: relative rest for 24–48 hours, then gradual return to usual activity below the symptom threshold; avoid climbing, contact play, cycling, and other fall-risk activities until recovered. Arrange primary-care follow-up in 1–2 weeks, sooner for persistent symptoms. <cite s="d6328e4e-f6fb-5613-8a42-228fe0f30d0b"/>

The key next action is transfer to an ED or observation-capable setting if this outpatient clinic cannot provide serial neurologic observation and rapid CT access.
