This presentation meets diagnostic criteria for AOM based on middle ear effusion plus acute inflammation, with bulging tympanic membrane as the hallmark finding. Management should prioritize pain control and then choose between initial observation versus immediate antibiotics based on risk and severity.

Initial management choice

This child is not in a high-risk group that requires immediate antibiotics (high-risk features include age under 6 months, immunocompromise, toxic appearance, or craniofacial abnormalities). For most non–high-risk children, initial observation with analgesia is suggested because many cases improve within about three days without antibiotics and antibiotics have modest absolute benefit with higher adverse effects.
+3

However, children who are younger than 2 years, have more severe manifestations (eg, high fever, severe pain, bilateral AOM, marked bulging, otorrhea), or have symptoms for more than 72 hours at presentation are more likely to benefit from immediate antibiotics. Children under 2 years with unilateral nonsevere AOM may still benefit from antibiotics, but the benefit is smaller than in bilateral or severe disease.

Analgesia plan

Use oral ibuprofen or acetaminophen for ear pain. Topical anesthetic ear drops are not appropriate here because topical benzocaine should not be used in children under 2 years.
+1

Observation “safety-net” plan

If observation is chosen, provide clear contingency instructions and either (a) a plan to obtain antibiotics quickly if needed, or (b) a delayed prescription to start only if the child worsens or does not improve within 72 hours. Examples of “worsening” that can trigger starting antibiotics or re-evaluation include fever above 39°C, pain not improved with ibuprofen or acetaminophen, lack of improvement within 72 hours, new ear drainage, or worsening fussiness.

Follow-up and complications to watch for

If symptoms worsen or fail to improve after 72 hours of observation, start antibiotics. Advise urgent reassessment for findings concerning for complications (eg, postauricular swelling or protrusion of the auricle, vestibular symptoms, cranial nerve deficits, meningeal signs, or focal neurologic findings).
