Assessment and differential diagnosis

The leading diagnosis is suspected bacterial CAP secondary to S. pneumoniae. Viral pneumonia is possible but less likely given the focal examination findings without wheeze or diffuse involvement. Important alternatives to exclude are:

Atypical pneumonia (Mycoplasma pneumoniae) — less likely at age 5 with focal consolidation, but consider if macrolide-resistant strains prevalent or if poor response to amoxicillin.
Pleural effusion or empyema — decreased breath sounds raise this possibility; chest imaging will clarify.
Foreign body aspiration — consider if history of choking, though less likely without acute onset.
Immediate actions

The child is hemodynamically stable without respiratory distress. Observation of oxygen saturation with ambulation and reassessment after an observation period in the clinic is appropriate.
