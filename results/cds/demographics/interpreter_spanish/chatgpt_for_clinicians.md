Assessment

Right acute otitis media, nonsevere. Moderate TM bulging with middle-ear effusion establishes AOM. At 24 months, with mild otalgia for <48 hours and temperature 38.7°C (<39°C), he meets criteria for nonsevere AOM. 
PEDIATRICS

Management

Observation/delayed antibiotic is a reasonable first choice if the mother is comfortable with it and reliable follow-up is available. For children ≥24 months with nonsevere unilateral or bilateral AOM, either observation or immediate antibiotics is acceptable; observation requires reassessment or rescue treatment if worsening or not improving within 48–72 hours. 
PEDIATRICS

Given that he is well appearing, drinking well, has unilateral disease, short-duration mild pain, and fever below 39°C, I would favor watchful waiting/shared decision-making rather than automatic antibiotics.

A delayed/safety-net prescription is another reasonable implementation. 
JAMA

Pain/fever control: acetaminophen or ibuprofen using standard pediatric weight-based dosing. Analgesia should be addressed whether or not antibiotics are prescribed.

If antibiotics are chosen now, or observation fails: first-line is amoxicillin 80–90 mg/kg/day PO divided twice daily, provided he has not received amoxicillin in the preceding 30 days, has no purulent conjunctivitis, and has no history of recurrent AOM unresponsive to amoxicillin. 
PEDIATRICS

For a child age 2–5 years with mild/moderate AOM, a 7-day course is appropriate. 
AAP

I would not hand-calculate the 12.4-kg dose here without an approved pediatric dosing calculator.

If recent amoxicillin, purulent conjunctivitis, or prior recurrent AOM unresponsive to amoxicillin is present, amoxicillin-clavulanate rather than plain amoxicillin is preferred. 
PEDIATRICS

Follow-up: reassess sooner for clinical deterioration; otherwise reassess if pain/fever is worsening or not clearly improving within 48–72 hours. New otorrhea, postauricular swelling/tenderness, auricular protrusion, toxic appearance, dehydration, or neurologic symptoms warrant prompt evaluation. 
PEDIATRICS

Because Spanish is the family's preferred language, I would conduct shared decision-making through the interpreter and provide Spanish-language medication/return instructions with teach-back.

Information that would change the antibiotic choice: amoxicillin exposure in the past 30 days, purulent conjunctivitis, and a history of recurrent AOM failing amoxicillin. Reliable access to follow-up is also necessary before choosing observation.
