Analyzed query, searched for evidence

Observation in clinic with a period of monitoring, rather than immediate CT, is the appropriate plan for this 24-month-old, who falls into the PECARN intermediate-risk group with a single isolated risk factor (one episode of vomiting) and an otherwise reassuring exam. At 24 months he is managed under the PECARN algorithm for children ≥2 years (the <2-year algorithm applies only to those younger than 2), so the occipital scalp hematoma — a risk factor only in the <2-year rule — does not itself drive imaging here.

Where he falls in PECARN (≥2 years)

Suggested plan

Observe in clinic for a period (≈4–6 hours from injury) with serial neuro checks and mental-status assessments. Isolated vomiting that resolves, with a child who remains alert and playful, argues strongly against ciTBI and supports avoiding CT and its radiation exposure.

Offer a trial of oral fluids to confirm he tolerates PO and is not persistently vomiting. Escalate to CT if vomiting recurs/becomes protracted, mental status changes, he becomes inconsolable or lethargic, develops a focal deficit, or a palpable/basilar skull fracture becomes apparent.
