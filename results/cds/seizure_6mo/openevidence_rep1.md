Why this is classified as complex

Febrile seizures are defined by fever ≥38°C without CNS infection or metabolic cause in a child 6–60 months old. A seizure is complex if it lasts ≥15 minutes, has focal features, or recurs within 24 hours.

Assess for meningitis/encephalitis — the central decision at 6 months

Bacterial CNS infection risk is very low with simple febrile seizures but higher with complex ones, and infants can have subtle/absent meningeal signs. AAP guidance is to strongly consider (and many recommend) lumbar puncture for febrile seizure in the 6–12 month window, particularly when Hib/pneumococcal immunization is incomplete/uncertain, if the child was pretreated with antibiotics, or if any meningismus or ill appearance is present.

Immediate workup

Identify the fever source: she has a viral URI (rhinorrhea, cough), the most common febrile-seizure trigger; a focused exam for otitis, and consideration of respiratory viral testing, is appropriate. Routine bloodwork/imaging is not required to evaluate the seizure itself.
