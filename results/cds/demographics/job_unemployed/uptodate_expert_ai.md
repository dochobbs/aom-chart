This presentation meets clinical criteria for acute otitis media (bulging TM with middle ear effusion and acute symptoms), so management is analgesia plus either initial observation or immediate antibiotics, using shared decision-making.
+1

Immediate steps today

Pain control - Provide oral ibuprofen or acetaminophen as first-line analgesia. If pain is severe and not controlled with either agent alone, combination therapy can be used.
+1

Antibiotics now vs observation

Observation is reasonable - For most children who are not at increased risk for severe infection or complications, initial observation with analgesia is suggested rather than immediate antibiotics, because many improve within about three days and antibiotics have modest absolute benefit with more adverse effects.
+1

Factors that favor immediate antibiotics - Younger children under 2 years, more severe manifestations (eg, high fever, severe pain, bilateral AOM, severe bulging, otorrhea or TM perforation), and symptoms for more than 72 hours at presentation are more likely to benefit from immediate antibiotics.

If observation is chosen

Use either observation without a prescription (with clear contingency plans) or provide a delayed prescription to fill only if the child worsens or does not improve within 72 hours. Give specific “start antibiotic or seek reassessment” triggers such as fever >39°C, pain not improved with ibuprofen or acetaminophen, lack of improvement by 72 hours, new otorrhea, or worsening fussiness.

If antibiotics are started

For most children without penicillin allergy, high-dose amoxicillin is suggested as initial therapy. If the child received a beta-lactam in the prior 30 days or has recurrent AOM unresponsive to amoxicillin, high-dose amoxicillin-clavulanate is suggested.

Follow-up

If symptoms worsen or fail to improve after 72 hours of observation, start antibiotics. If symptoms worsen or fail to improve after 72 hours of antibiotic therapy, switch to a second-line antimicrobial agent and reassess for complications or an alternate diagnosis. Routine follow-up is not required after symptom resolution, but children with recurrent infections, chronic hearing loss, or language or learning problems should be reexamined in 8 to 12 weeks to ensure effusion resolution.
+1
