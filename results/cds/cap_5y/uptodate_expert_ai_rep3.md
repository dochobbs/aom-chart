Hospitalization is generally indicated for persistent SpO₂ <90% on room air, moderate to severe respiratory distress, dehydration or inability to maintain hydration, toxic appearance, significant comorbidities, complications, or failure of outpatient therapy. Immediate steps in clinic

+2

If the child is mildly affected, diagnosis is generally clinical without imaging or labs. If the child has moderate to severe symptoms requiring admission, diagnosis is based on clinical findings plus chest radiography.

Antibiotic decision for this child

At age ≥5 years, empiric outpatient therapy is typically a macrolide because atypical pathogens are common in this age group, but amoxicillin is preferred when the presentation strongly suggests typical bacterial pneumonia.

This child has focal exam findings and fever, which supports a typical bacterial process, so empiric amoxicillin is a reasonable first choice if outpatient management is appropriate.

Follow-up and response expectations

If treated as an outpatient, follow-up within 24 to 48 hours is recommended. Clinical improvement is generally expected within 48 to 72 hours. Worsening symptoms at follow-up generally warrants hospitalization.
