AAP 2013 guidelines classify unilateral acute otitis media in children 24 months and older as eligible for either antibiotic treatment or observation with pain management, provided symptoms are not severe. Evaluating high-dose amoxicillin dosing in AOM

High-dose amoxicillin 80–90 mg/kg/day divided BID is recommended for unilateral acute otitis media in children 24 months and older with moderate symptoms, based on AAP 2013 guidelines. For a 12.4 kg child, this translates to 496–558 mg per dose, with 500 mg BID using 400 mg/5 mL suspension as a practical formulation. Confirm appropriate dosing and monitor for gastrointestinal side effects and allergic reactions.

Management plan

Analgesia (initiate immediately regardless of antibiotic decision)

Acetaminophen 10–15 mg/kg orally every 4–6 hours as needed (maximum 75 mg/kg/day, not to exceed 3,000 mg/day).
Ibuprofen 10 mg/kg orally every 6–8 hours as needed (if ≥6 months old) as an alternative or for breakthrough pain.

Antibiotic therapy (first-line for uncomplicated AOM)

Amoxicillin 80–90 mg/kg/day divided twice daily for 7–10 days.
Weight-based dose: 12.4 kg × 80–90 mg/kg = approximately 1,000 mg/day.
Practical dosing: 400 mg orally twice daily (using 400 mg/5 mL suspension: 5 mL per dose) for 7–10 days.
Alternative: 500 mg twice daily if using 250 mg/5 mL or 400 mg/5 mL suspension (6.25 mL per dose). 
Pediatrics

When to use amoxicillin-clavulanate instead

If the child had amoxicillin within the past 30 days, has purulent conjunctivitis (suggestive of H. influenzae), or local resistance patterns warrant broader coverage. In that case: high-dose amoxicillin-clavulanate 90 mg/kg/day (based on amoxicillin component) divided BID.

Observation option (if parents prefer and reliable follow-up is assured)

For children ≥24 months with unilateral AOM and nonsevere symptoms, observation with close follow-up and a "safety-net" prescription is acceptable. The child must be re-evaluated within 48–72 hours; start antibiotics if symptoms worsen or fail to improve. 
Pediatrics
 Ensure parents understand return precautions.

Follow-up and monitoring

Reassess within 48–72 hours if symptoms persist, or sooner if worsening.
For children treated with observation: telephone follow-up within 48 hours is recommended to ensure improvement; fill the antibiotic prescription if no improvement or worsening symptoms.

Red flags requiring urgent re-evaluation

Persistent or worsening fever beyond 48–72 hours.
Development of otorrhea (ear drainage).
Severe or increasing ear pain, or pain unresponsive to analgesics.
Decreased oral intake, signs of dehydration, or lethargy/irritability.
Postauricular swelling or tenderness (mastoiditis).
Stiff neck, severe headache, or altered mental status.

Treatment failure (defined as persistent or worsening symptoms after 48–72 hours of antibiotics)

Change to amoxicillin-clavulanate if amoxicillin was first-line.
If already on amoxicillin-clavulanate or severe symptoms: consider ceftriaxone 50 mg/kg IM/IV daily for 3 days, or tympanocentesis for culture if available. 
Clin Pediatr

Counseling points

Complete the full antibiotic course if initiated; do not save antibiotics for future use.
Ear pain typically improves within 24–48 hours with analgesia; fever should resolve within 48–72 hours with antibiotics.
The child may return to daycare when fever-free and feeling well enough to participate.

Prevention

Ensure pneumococcal vaccination remains up to date (already documented as current).
Avoid tobacco smoke exposure.
Hand hygiene to reduce viral URI transmission.
