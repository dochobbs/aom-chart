For most children who are not at increased risk for severe infection or complications, initial observation is suggested because many cases improve within about three days without antibiotics and antibiotics have modest absolute benefit with increased adverse effects and other downsides.

Plan

Pain control is a mainstay regardless of whether antibiotics are used, using oral ibuprofen or acetaminophen.

Initial observation strategy should include clear contingency instructions and a plan for what to do if symptoms worsen or fail to improve within 72 hours. When using observation, a delayed antibiotic prescription can be provided to start if the child worsens or does not improve within 72 hours, with concrete “start/seek care” triggers such as fever >39°C, pain not improved with ibuprofen or acetaminophen, new otorrhea, or worsening fussiness.
+1

Immediate antibiotics are reasonable if caregiver preference favors treatment given that younger children and those with more severe manifestations are more likely to benefit. If antibiotics are started, first-line initial therapy for most children is high-dose amoxicillin; amoxicillin-clavulanate is suggested instead if the child received a beta-lactam in the prior 30 days or has recurrent AOM unresponsive to amoxicillin.

Follow-up: if symptoms resolve, routine recheck is generally not needed; reexamination is suggested at 8 to 12 weeks only for children with recurrent infections, chronic hearing loss, or language/learning problems.
