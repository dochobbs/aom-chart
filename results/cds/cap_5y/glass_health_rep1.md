Because SpO₂ is 93% with tachypnea and focal findings, I would repeat the saturation carefully after antipyresis and rest, assess its persistence/trajectory, and have a low threshold for same-day emergency evaluation rather than routine outpatient treatment. Hospitalization is generally indicated below 90%, but some children at 93% require admission depending on sustained hypoxemia, work of breathing, hydration, and reliable observation. 

Immediate assessment

Repeat manual respiratory rate when afebrile and calm; repeat pulse oximetry with a well-perfused finger, good waveform, and ideally a second site/device.
Observe for 20–30 minutes, including ambulation/talking if safe; reassess work of breathing, mental status, perfusion, oral intake, and saturation.
Obtain a chest radiograph today because of focal decreased breath sounds/crackles and borderline oxygenation, particularly to evaluate consolidation or parapneumonic effusion.
Consider viral testing only if it will change isolation, treatment, or disposition; a positive viral result does not exclude bacterial pneumonia when focal findings and hypoxemia are present.

Disposition

Send to the ED for oxygen assessment and likely imaging/observation if SpO₂ remains ≤93% or falls, if tachypnea persists or worsens, if retractions/grunting/cyanosis develops, if she cannot maintain hydration, or if reliable close observation is unavailable. Any clinical deterioration warrants urgent transfer. A stable saturation clearly above 93–94%, no increased work of breathing, normal perfusion, adequate intake, and reliable follow-up could support outpatient management with reassessment within 24 hours.

If outpatient treatment is appropriate

Start amoxicillin 90 mg/kg/day PO divided twice daily: for 18.5 kg, approximately 830 mg PO every 12 hours; use the formulation’s measurable equivalent and local maximum-dose policy.
Treat for the shortest duration supported by the current local pediatric CAP protocol; reassess clinically within 24 hours given the saturation and respiratory rate.
Provide fluids, rest, and weight-based antipyretic therapy; avoid cough/cold combination products.
Return immediately for increased work of breathing, blue lips, inability to drink, repeated vomiting, lethargy, confusion, or worsening fever/respiratory status.
