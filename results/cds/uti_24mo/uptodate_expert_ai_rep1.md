Disposition and initial actions

Outpatient management is appropriate for most children older than one month who are not vomiting and can take oral medication, as long as close follow-up is possible. Hospitalization and parenteral therapy are generally reserved for clinical urosepsis, immunocompromise, vomiting or inability to tolerate oral medication, lack of reliable follow-up, or failure to respond to outpatient therapy.
+1

Empiric antibiotics now, then culture-directed adjustment

Urine for culture and susceptibility should be collected before starting empiric therapy. Given fever and urinalysis findings (nitrite positive, leukocyte esterase, pyuria, bacteriuria), empiric therapy should be started immediately.
+1

For outpatient empiric therapy in children 1 month to 2 years, a first-generation oral cephalosporin is recommended first-line when local resistance of E. coli to first-generation cephalosporins is not high. Total antibiotic duration for febrile UTI is typically 10 days.
+1

When culture results return, definitive therapy should be based on organism identification and susceptibilities. If the organism is resistant to the empiric agent, some experts change therapy, while others do not if the child is clinically improving.
+1

Expected clinical course and what to do if not improving

Most children improve clinically within 24 to 48 hours of appropriate therapy. If clinical condition (other than persistent fever) worsens or fails to improve as expected within 48 to 72 hours, hospital admission for parenteral therapy is recommended, and kidney and bladder ultrasonography should be performed as soon as possible to evaluate for kidney abscess.
+1

Imaging after this first febrile UTI

Kidney and bladder ultrasonography is suggested after a first febrile UTI in young children, with the option to defer if prenatal ultrasonography performed at a reputable center at >30 to 32 weeks gestation was normal and results are accessible.
+1

Voiding cystourethrogram is generally reserved for children with ≥2 febrile UTIs, or after a first febrile UTI if kidney ultrasound is abnormal, the pathogen is not E. coli, or there is poor growth or hypertension.
+1

Follow-up counseling

Caregivers should be instructed to seek prompt evaluation for subsequent febrile illnesses, and evaluation should include urinalysis and urine culture. Primary care follow-up should include monitoring of height, weight, and blood pressure.
