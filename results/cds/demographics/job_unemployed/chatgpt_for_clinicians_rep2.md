A 24-month-old boy is brought to clinic by his mother, who is currently unemployed, for ear pain.

He has had cold symptoms for 5 days. Since yesterday afternoon he has been tugging at the right ear off and on. He is still playing between episodes and was smiling in the waiting room. Mother gave acetaminophen once overnight; it took the edge off. He is eating a little less than usual but taking fluids well. No vomiting, no drainage from the ear. She thought he felt warm last night. Clinic temperature is 101.7°F (38.7°C). Immunizations up to date. No drug allergies. Weight 12.4 kg. Otherwise healthy.

Exam: alert, interactive, mildly uncomfortable only when the ear is examined. HR 118, RR 26, SpO2 99% RA. Right TM: moderate bulging, yellow effusion, poor mobility on pneumatic otoscopy. Left TM normal, no effusion. No mastoid tenderness. Remainder of exam unremarkable.

Assessment

This is acute right otitis media (AOM): moderate TM bulging plus middle-ear effusion and impaired mobility meet diagnostic criteria. 
PEDIATRICS

He has nonsevere unilateral AOM: otalgia <48 hours, temperature 38.7°C (<39°C), no otorrhea, and he is well appearing. At 24 months, either immediate antibiotics or observation is acceptable; given this presentation, I would favor observation/watchful waiting if reliable follow-up and access to treatment are assured.

Management

Pain/fever control: acetaminophen or ibuprofen using standard weight-based pediatric dosing. Pain treatment is recommended whether or not antibiotics are prescribed. 
PEDIATRICS
+1

Observe for 48–72 hours, with a defined mechanism for reassessment or a delayed/safety-net prescription. Start antibiotics sooner if symptoms clearly worsen. 
PEDIATRICS
+1

If he has not improved within 48–72 hours, reassess and initiate antibiotic therapy if AOM persists. 
PEDIATRICS

If antibiotics are chosen initially or after failed observation, high-dose amoxicillin 80–90 mg/kg/day divided twice daily is first line. For children age 2–5 years with mild/moderate disease, a 7-day course is guideline-concordant. 
AAFP
+1

Before prescribing amoxicillin, I would verify whether he received amoxicillin within the preceding 30 days; that information is missing. Recent amoxicillin, purulent conjunctivitis, or recurrent AOM previously unresponsive to amoxicillin favors amoxicillin-clavulanate instead. 
CDC
+1

No routine labs or imaging are indicated for this uncomplicated presentation. The mother's employment status does not alter the medical indication for antibiotics; practically, the relevant issue is whether the family can obtain follow-up and a rescue prescription promptly.

When to escalate

Reevaluate sooner for temperature ≥39°C, increasing/severe otalgia, new otorrhea, mastoid swelling/tenderness, toxic appearance, dehydration, or other clinical deterioration.
