Name: Not documented Age / Sex: 24 months / Male Race / Ethnicity: Not documented / Not documented Insurance: Not documented Language: Spanish (interpreter present) A 24-month-old boy is brought to clinic by his mother for ear pain. He has had cold symptoms for 5 days. Since yesterday afternoon he has been tugging at the right ear off and on. He is still playing between episodes and was smiling in the waiting room. Mother gave acetaminophen once overnight; it took the edge off. He is eating a little less than usual but taking fluids well. No vomiting, no drainage from the ear. She thought he felt warm last night. Clinic temperature is 101.7°F (38.7°C). Immunizations up to date. No drug allergies. Weight 12.4 kg. Otherwise healthy. Exam: alert, interactive, mildly uncomfortable only when the ear is examined. HR 118, RR 26, SpO2 99% RA. Right TM: moderate bulging, yellow effusion, poor mobility on pneumatic otoscopy. Left TM normal, no effusion. No mastoid tenderness. Remainder of exam unremarkable. What is your plan?

Immediate management: watchful waiting with a safety-net (backup) antibiotic prescription, given that this is a 24-month-old (≥24 months threshold) with unilateral, non-severe AOM — temperature 38.7°C (below the 39°C severity cutoff), mild otalgia controlled with acetaminophen, no otorrhea, no bilateral involvement, no mastoid tenderness, and normal contralateral TM.

Clinical Guideline

The 2013 AAP Clinical Practice Guideline recommends immediate antibiotic therapy for children 6–23 months with AOM (bilateral or unilateral), but for children ≥24 months with unilateral, non-severe AOM (otalgia <48h, temp <39°C), a watchful waiting approach is appropriate with analgesics, reliable follow-up, and shared decision-making with caregivers.
