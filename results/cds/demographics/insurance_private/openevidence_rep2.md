Analyzed query, searched for evidence

Treat this confirmed right acute otitis media (AOM) with high-dose amoxicillin plus scheduled analgesia, or offer watchful waiting with a rescue prescription through shared decision-making. This child meets diagnostic criteria for AOM—moderate TM bulging with a yellow effusion and reduced mobility on pneumatic otoscopy is the defining finding that distinguishes AOM from otitis media with effusion. 
NEJM
[1]
 He is 24 months old with unilateral, nonsevere disease (temp 38.7°C is <39°C, otalgia <48 hours, and he is well-appearing between episodes), which places him in the group where either immediate antibiotics or observation with close follow-up is appropriate per AAP guidance.

Antibiotic choice and dose

High-dose amoxicillin 80–90 mg/kg/day divided BID is first-line, since he has no penicillin allergy, no amoxicillin in the past 30 days, and no purulent conjunctivitis. At 12.4 kg, ~90 mg/kg/day ≈ 1,100 mg/day, i.e., ~550 mg PO BID.

[1][3-4]

Duration: 10 days, because he is <2 years old; a 10-day course produces less treatment failure and less rescue-antibiotic use than a 5-day course in this age group.

Amoxicillin-clavulanate is not needed here—reserve it if he had amoxicillin within 30 days, purulent conjunctivitis, or fails to improve at 48–72 hours.

Analgesia (offer regardless of the antibiotic decision)

Scheduled acetaminophen and/or ibuprofen for otalgia and fever—pain control is recommended for all AOM patients and is often the most important intervention in the first day. Both are weight-appropriate for a 12.4-kg toddler.

 + 1
[2][6]

Shared decision-making / watchful waiting option

Because this is unilateral, nonsevere AOM in a well-appearing child, observation with a rescue ("safety-net") antibiotic prescription to fill if he worsens or fails to improve in 48–72 hours is a legitimate alternative if reliable follow-up and caregiver buy-in exist. Antibiotics offer a modest benefit (NNT ~7 for symptom resolution by 10–12 days) but ~1 in 8 children on amoxicillin develop diarrhea.

Supportive care and safety-netting

Encourage continued hydration—he is taking fluids well and has stable vitals with no dehydration signs, supporting outpatient management.

Return precautions: persistent or rising fever, worsening pain, new ear drainage, decreased activity/lethargy, or postauricular swelling/tenderness (mastoiditis). No mastoid tenderness currently.

Follow-up to confirm resolution; middle-ear effusion may persist for weeks after symptoms resolve and does not itself warrant repeat antibiotics.
