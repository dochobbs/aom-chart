A 24-month-old boy is brought to clinic by his mother for ear pain.

He has had cold symptoms for 5 days. Since yesterday afternoon he has been tugging at the right ear off and on. He is still playing between episodes and was smiling in the waiting room. Mother gave acetaminophen once overnight; it took the edge off. He is eating a little less than usual but taking fluids well. No vomiting, no drainage from the ear. She thought he felt warm last night. Clinic temperature is 101.7°F (38.7°C). Immunizations up to date. No drug allergies. Weight 12.4 kg. Otherwise healthy.

Exam: alert, interactive, mildly uncomfortable only when the ear is examined. HR 118, RR 26, SpO2 99% RA. Right TM: moderate bulging, yellow effusion, poor mobility on pneumatic otoscopy. Left TM normal, no effusion. No mastoid tenderness. Remainder of exam unremarkable.

Assessment

Right acute otitis media (AOM), unilateral and nonsevere. Moderate TM bulging plus middle-ear effusion/poor mobility meets diagnostic criteria for AOM. His otalgia has been mild and <48 hours, temperature is 38.7°C (<39°C), and there is no otorrhea or mastoid finding. 
CDC

Management

Observation with adequate analgesia is a reasonable default, using shared decision-making with the mother. At 24 months, a child with unilateral nonsevere AOM may either be observed or treated immediately with antibiotics, provided reliable reassessment/treatment can occur if symptoms worsen or fail to improve within 48–72 hours. 
American Family Physician

DailyMed

If immediate antibiotics are preferred, or observation fails: use high-dose amoxicillin 80–90 mg/kg/day PO divided BID. For a nonsevere child ≥2 years, a 5–7 day course is supported; I would generally use 7 days at exactly age 2. 
American Family Physician
+1

Before finalizing amoxicillin, confirm two items not explicitly documented:

any amoxicillin exposure within the previous 30 days

concurrent purulent conjunctivitis
Either would favor amoxicillin-clavulanate rather than amoxicillin alone. 
CDC

No routine labs, cultures, or imaging are indicated for this uncomplicated presentation. Reexamine if symptoms worsen or have not clearly improved within 48–72 hours; persistent AOM after appropriate amoxicillin generally warrants escalation of therapy. 
American Family Physician

Seek prompt reassessment for postauricular swelling/tenderness, protrusion of the ear, toxic appearance, persistent/high fever, severe or increasing pain, new otorrhea, poor oral intake/dehydration, or neurologic symptoms.

My choice here: analgesia + 48–72-hour observation/SNAP, assuming the family has reliable access to follow-up. Immediate amoxicillin is also guideline-concordant if the parent prefers treatment after discussing the modest benefit versus adverse effects.
