Manage as outpatient bacterial community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) with high-dose oral amoxicillin, provided oxygenation stays acceptable and follow-up is reliable — this is the most likely diagnosis given the 5-year-old's focal right-base crackles, decreased breath sounds, fever, and tachypnea. She is appropriately immunized, alert, speaking in full sentences, feeding somewhat but drinking well, with capillary refill under 2 seconds and no retractions or nasal flaring, all of which favor outpatient care.

Disposition — the SpO2 is the pivotal point

IDSA lists hypoxemia (SpO2 <90–92%) as an indication for hospitalization, along with respiratory distress, dehydration, inability to tolerate oral intake, and inability of caregivers to comply with therapy or follow-up. At 93% she is just above that band, but a single borderline reading warrants confirmation.

First-line antibiotic (previously healthy, fully immunized, ≥5 years)

Amoxicillin 90 mg/kg/day divided twice daily is first-line for presumed bacterial CAP, covering S. pneumoniae. At 18.5 kg this is ≈1665 mg/day → ~830 mg PO twice daily (round to a practical formulation dose).

Duration: 5 days. The SAFER RCT and a subsequent meta-analysis show short-course (3–5 day) therapy is non-inferior to 10 days for non-severe pediatric CAP, and outpatient courses need not exceed 7 days.

Chest radiography

A CXR is not mandatory for a straightforward outpatient CAP diagnosis, but is reasonable/indicated here given hypoxemia and a focal exam — it helps confirm the infiltrate and screen for a parapneumonic effusion/empyema, which the decreased breath sounds at the right base could suggest. Obtain PA and lateral views; add a lateral decubitus or ultrasound if effusion is suspected.

Supportive care and caregiver counseling

Antipyretics/analgesia (continue ibuprofen and/or acetaminophen) and encourage oral hydration; she is drinking well, which is reassuring.

Avoid mucolytics — they do not improve outcomes.

Safety-netting for the mother: return immediately for increasing work of breathing (retractions, flaring, grunting), fast or labored breathing, bluish color, lethargy or difficulty rousing, poor drinking/reduced urine output/signs of dehydration, or persistent high fever.

Follow-up

Reassess in 48–72 hours. Children not improving in that window need re-evaluation
