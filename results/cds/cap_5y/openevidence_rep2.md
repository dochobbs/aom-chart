Analyzed query, searched for evidence

Outpatient management with high-dose oral amoxicillin is appropriate for this previously healthy, fully immunized 5-year-old with focal right basilar crackles, fever, and tachypnea consistent with community-acquired pneumonia (CAP)—provided the borderline oxygen saturation is confirmed and observed. She is well-appearing, hydrated, tolerating fluids, speaking in full sentences, and has no retractions or nasal flaring, which supports non-severe disease that can be treated at home with close follow-up.

 Recheck saturation on a good waveform; a transient reading during agitation or a cool extremity is common. Persistent SpO₂ ≤92% or any evolving respiratory distress shifts her toward admission.

Assessment

Findings favoring bacterial (typical) CAP: focal crackles with decreased breath sounds at one lobe, fever, and anorexia. In a fully immunized 5-year-old the leading treatable pathogen is S. pneumoniae; atypicals (M. pneumoniae) become more relevant with age but usually present more diffusely.

Findings favoring non-severe/outpatient: alert, full sentences, no retractions/flaring/grunting, cap refill <2 s, drinking well, RR 38 (just below the 1–5 yr WHO cutoff of >40).

Antibiotics (first-line)

High-dose amoxicillin 90 mg/kg/day — for 18.5 kg ≈ 1665 mg/day, e.g., ~830 mg PO BID (or divided TID). 
CID
[2]

Duration: 5 days, supported by the SAFER RCT and meta-analysis showing short courses non-inferior to 10 days in non-hospitalized children. Do not exceed 7 days for uncomplicated outpatient CAP.

[1][6-7]

Macrolides are not needed empirically here; reserve azithromycin if atypical features emerge or poor response.

[2]

Supportive care and counseling

Continue antipyretics (ibuprofen/acetaminophen) and emphasize hydration; mucolytics are not helpful. 
AFP
[1]

Consider a rapid influenza test if influenza is circulating locally, since oseltamivir would be indicated for moderate-to-severe influenza-associated CAP.

Imaging/labs

Chest radiography is not required to confirm outpatient CAP but is reasonable given borderline hypoxemia, or reserve it for failure to improve within 48–72 hours or diagnostic uncertainty. 
AFP
[1]

Return precautions / follow-up

Reassess in 48–72 hours (in person or by phone); if no improvement, reconsider atypical pathogen, complication (effusion/empyema), or alternative diagnosis.
