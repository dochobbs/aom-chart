The American College of Radiology (ACR) supports risk stratification by the PECARN criteria and, for a child at intermediate risk, favors structured clinical observation as a reasonable alternative to CT.

Recommended plan: observe in clinic rather than obtain immediate CT. This well-appearing 24-month-old sustained a low-height fall with a single episode of vomiting, an occipital scalp hematoma, and an entirely normal neurologic exam (GCS 15, non-focal, normal gait, reactive pupils, no signs of skull fracture). Under the PECARN ≥2-year rule, his only risk factor is isolated vomiting, placing him in the intermediate-risk category (~0.8% risk of clinically important TBI), where observation is preferred over CT for most children.

Key reasoning on PECARN application:

At 24 months he crosses into the ≥2-year algorithm. In this rule the relevant predictors are altered mental status/GCS <15, any LOC, vomiting, signs of basilar skull fracture, severe headache, and severe mechanism. A fall from a couch is not a severe mechanism (which for this age requires a fall >1.5 m).

Isolated vomiting carries a low ciTBI risk; in the PREDICT validation, the intermediate-risk group had a ciTBI rate of only 0.7% in children ≥2 years, and risk rises mainly with multiple predictors rather than one.

Note that the occipital scalp hematoma, a risk factor in the <2-year rule, is not a predictor in the ≥2-year rule, so it does not independently drive imaging here.
