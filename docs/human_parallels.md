# Human parallels — the error modes in the human literature

Tracked citation bank for the blog post, talk, and posts. Every citation here was verified against its source on **2026-08-22** (author, venue, year, and the specific number quoted). Add nothing to this file without checking the source first; the one rejection at the bottom is why.

## Mode-by-mode map

| Mode | Our finding (tiebroken finals) | Human parallel | Citation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 Omission** | Barely fired — 3/140. Model plans are thorough to a fault. | Humans omit constantly: US adults received **54.9%** of recommended care across 439 indicators. The models' failure profile is inverted from ours. | McGlynn et al., *NEJM* 2003 |
| **2 Invented facts** | 57/140 rows (41%) stated ≥1 fact never in the chart; follow-up asserted 11/140; Fable flat no-recent-abx 13/14 | Only **38.5%** of documented review-of-systems and **53.2%** of documented physical exam confirmed against audio/observation of the actual encounter ("WNL = we never looked") | Berdahl et al., *JAMA Network Open* 2019 |
| **2 (propagation)** | An invented premise, once emitted, is stated with the same confidence as charted fact | **82%** of ICU resident notes (74% attending) carry ≥20% text copied from the prior day; copied errors persist | Thornton et al., *Critical Care Medicine* 2013 |
| **3 Harmful commission** | 0/140 — untestable by design (treat and observe both legal) | Abundantly measured in humans even though our stem can't reach it: adverse events in **23.6% of hospital admissions**, ~23% of them preventable, 32% serious | Bates et al., *NEJM* 2023 |
| **4 Citation failure** | 11/140: "AAP requires antibiotics" attached to the wrong age band | **16.9%** of quotations in medical literature don't accurately support the claim they're attached to (~8% major errors; earlier estimate 25.4%) | Pober/2025 meta, *Res Integr Peer Rev*; Jergas & Baethge, *PeerJ* 2015 |
| **5 Stale guidance** | Haiku: 45 mg/kg amoxicillin (superseded 2004) on 10/14 | Physician adherence to current standards of care declines with years since training on most measures | Choudhry et al., *Annals of Internal Medicine* 2005 |
| **6 Finite-rule error** | Sonnet: stated 24-month-old binned "under 2" on 12/14; fever drift 101.7°F → "≥39°C"; one dose-per-day error | Left-digit bias: AMI patients 2 weeks past their 80th birthday ~24% less likely to receive CABG than 2 weeks before it | Olenski et al., *NEJM* 2020 |
| **7 Bias** | Prescriptions never moved (2 pre-registered nulls). Justifications and defaults did: Fable nurse-as-WW-reason 6/6 vs 0/6; Flash WW-"Preferred" 5/6 private vs 1/6 Medicaid; Haiku unemployment-as-reliability-doubt 3/6 | Race/SES shaped physicians' perceptions of intelligence, adherence, and follow-up likelihood after real encounters, independent of visible decisions; Black patients had **2.54×** odds of a negative descriptor ("noncompliant," "resistant") in 40,113 H&P notes | van Ryn & Burke, *Soc Sci Med* 2000; Sun et al., *Health Affairs* 2022 |

| "Misremembering the finding, the journal, the year" — clinicians' recall of what studies showed | **2/4** | Across 48 studies and 13,011 clinicians, the majority correctly estimated treatment **benefits in 11%** of outcomes and **harms in 13%** — systematically overestimating benefit, underestimating harm (Hoffmann & Del Mar, *JAMA Intern Med* 2017). ## Third harvest — Doc's mode-by-mode push (2026-08-22)

Doc's framing per mode → verified same-day. His rule for mode 1 governs the whole set: *"we are humanizing this, not looking for an axe to grind."*

| Mode | His framing | Verified literature |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | "Doctors rushed and stressed must cut things often" | It's arithmetic, not character: delivering all guideline-recommended primary care to an average panel would take **26.7 hours per day** (14.1 preventive + 7.2 chronic + 2.2 acute + 3.2 documentation); team-based care still needs 9.3 (Porter et al., *J Gen Intern Med* 2022). Preventive care alone: 7.4 h/day (Yarnall et al., *AJPH* 2003). Omission is the *rational* response to an impossible day. |
| **2** | "Human memory is so bad — overlap it with practice" | Patients forget **40–80%** of what clinicians tell them immediately; nearly half of what they retain is wrong (Kessels, *J R Soc Med* 2003). Clinician-side telephone game: after five verbal-only handover cycles, **2.5%** of patient information survived — vs 99% with a printed sheet (Bhabra et al., *Ann R Coll Surg Engl* 2007). |
| **3** | "A million articles on iatrogenic harm" | Bates 2023 (now in the main table): adverse events in 23.6% of admissions. |
| **4** | "False claims and false literature — again bad memory" | Same root as mode 2; Hoffmann & Del Mar (11%/13% correct) is the quantitative bridge. No new citation needed. |
| **5** | "So common we invented board certification and CME" | The profession's own knowledge base rots on schedule: of 363 NEJM articles testing an existing standard of care over a decade, **146 (40.2%) reversed the practice** (Prasad et al., *Mayo Clin Proc* 2013). MOC and CME are the institutional admission that mode 5 is universal. |
| **6** | "Math in your head; approximation near the cutoff" | The measurement itself gets rounded before it ever meets the threshold: **terminal-digit preference** — zero as the recorded end digit in ~45–64% of blood pressures (expected: 10%), and practices with heavier rounding record systematically lower BPs, changing treatment (Am J Hypertens 2006; multiple settings). Pair with Olenski for what happens after the rounding. |
| **7** | "Humans are racist with loads of stereotypes — low-hanging fruit" | **Half** of a sample of white medical students and residents endorsed at least one false biological belief ("Black skin is thicker"); endorsers rated Black patients' pain lower and made less accurate treatment recommendations (Hoffman et al., *PNAS* 2016). Non-endorsers showed no bias — it's the belief, not the profession. |

## Considered and rejected

- **Dresselhaus TR, Peabody JW, et al.** *JGIM* 2000 — almost cited for mode 2 (over-documentation); on verification it shows the **opposite**: chart abstraction *under*-estimated delivered preventive care by 16%. Charts omit real care as well as inventing absent care; this paper is the omission direction. Kept here as the reminder to verify before citing — which is also mode 4's whole point.
