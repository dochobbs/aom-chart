A 24-month-old boy is brought to clinic by his 19-year-old mother for ear pain.

He has had cold symptoms for 5 days. Since yesterday afternoon he has been tugging at the right ear off and on. He is still playing between episodes and was smiling in the waiting room. Mother gave acetaminophen once overnight; it took the edge off. He is eating a little less than usual but taking fluids well. No vomiting, no drainage from the ear. She thought he felt warm last night. Clinic temperature is 101.7°F (38.7°C). Immunizations up to date. No drug allergies. Weight 12.4 kg. Otherwise healthy.

Exam: alert, interactive, mildly uncomfortable only when the ear is examined. HR 118, RR 26, SpO2 99% RA. Right TM: moderate bulging, yellow effusion, poor mobility on pneumatic otoscopy. Left TM normal, no effusion. No mastoid tenderness. Remainder of exam unremarkable.

Assessment

This is right-sided, uncomplicated acute otitis media (AOM), nonsevere: moderate TM bulging plus middle-ear effusion/poor mobility establishes the diagnosis; fever is below 39°C, otalgia is mild and <48 hours, there is no otorrhea, and he is well appearing.

Management

Observation is a reasonable initial choice. At exactly 24 months, a child with unilateral or bilateral nonsevere AOM may be managed with either immediate antibiotics or observation after shared decision-making, provided reliable follow-up is available. 
PEDIATRICS
+2

If observing, use a 48–72-hour watchful-waiting/safety-net plan: treat pain, and start antibiotics if symptoms clearly worsen or fail to improve during that interval. A delayed prescription is one reasonable implementation. 
PEDIATRICS
+2

Analgesia is important regardless of antibiotic use—acetaminophen or ibuprofen as appropriate for age/weight.
