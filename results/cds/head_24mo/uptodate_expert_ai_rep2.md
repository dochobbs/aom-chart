This child is not low risk by PECARN (nonfrontal scalp hematoma and vomiting), so the default plan is ED-style observation for 4 to 6 hours post-injury, with head CT if symptoms worsen or fail to improve during observation.

Intermediate-risk features for ciTBI in children younger than 2 years include nonfrontal scalp hematoma and vomiting, and UpToDate suggests close observation for 4 to 6 hours with neuroimaging for those who do not improve or who worsen. This approach balances the low absolute risk of ciTBI against CT radiation risk.

Observation plan

Perform serial neurologic and symptom reassessments during the observation window, and obtain neuroimaging if new, persistent, or worsening symptoms develop.
+1

Disposition if observation remains reassuring

If symptoms improve during observation and the child meets discharge criteria, discharge home rather than admit. Discharge criteria include: no suspicion of inflicted injury, normal neurologic exam and baseline function, tolerating oral intake, no extracranial injuries requiring admission, and reliable caregivers who can observe and return if needed.

Discharge instructions and return precautions

Provide explicit caregiver instructions for monitoring and reasons to seek immediate care, including inability to awaken as instructed, continued vomiting or vomiting that begins or continues 4 to 6 hours after injury, change in mental status/behavior, unsteady gait/incoordination, or seizure.
