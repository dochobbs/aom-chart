ED evaluation focus

A lumbar puncture is required if there are clinical signs suggesting meningitis or encephalitis (eg, persistent altered consciousness, nuchal rigidity, bulging fontanelle, petechial rash), and it may be considered with a lower threshold in infants <12 months when concern persists or immunizations are not current or verified. Bacterial meningitis in infants and children (excluding neonates): Clinical features and diagnosis
+2

Initial labs commonly obtained for complex febrile seizures include glucose and basic chemistries (electrolytes, calcium, urea nitrogen), with additional fever-source evaluation guided by history and exam.

Urgent neuroimaging is reserved for children with concerning features such as persistently abnormal neurologic exam or signs of increased intracranial pressure or space-occupying lesion.

Disposition

Hospitalization is considered for complex febrile seizure, concern for CNS infection, persistent altered mental status or focal deficit, severity of the febrile illness, ability to ensure outpatient follow-up, and caregiver comfort.

Follow-up planning after ED assessment

If she remains at baseline with a nonfocal exam, outpatient EEG and brain MRI can be considered in consultation with pediatric neurology for risk stratification, but are usually not needed acutely.
