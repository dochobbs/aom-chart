Initial management

Pain management - Pain control is a mainstay of AOM care, and oral ibuprofen or acetaminophen are suggested rather than other analgesics.

Observation option (reasonable here) - For most children who are not at increased risk for severe infection or complications, initial observation with analgesia is suggested rather than immediate antibiotics. If observation is chosen, caregivers should receive explicit return precautions and a plan to start antibiotics or be re-evaluated if symptoms worsen or fail to improve within 72 hours. Examples of “worsening” that can be used for contingency planning include fever >39°C, pain not improved with ibuprofen or acetaminophen, new otorrhea, worsening fussiness, or lack of improvement by 72 hours. A delayed antibiotic prescription is an acceptable observation strategy.
+2

When immediate antibiotics would be preferred - Immediate antibiotics are recommended for children at increased risk (eg, age <6 months, immunocompromised, toxic appearance, craniofacial abnormalities). Immediate antibiotics are also more likely to benefit children with more severe manifestations (eg, high fever, severe pain, bilateral AOM, severe bulging, otorrhea, tympanic membrane perforation) or symptoms for >72 hours at presentation.
+1

Follow-up and complications to watch for

Most children do not need routine follow-up once symptoms resolve, but children with recurrent infections, chronic hearing loss, or language or learning problems should be reexamined in 8 to 12 weeks to ensure effusion resolution. Caregivers should be counseled to seek urgent evaluation for findings concerning for complications such as postauricular swelling or auricle protrusion (mastoiditis), vestibular symptoms, cranial nerve palsies, or meningeal signs or focal neurologic findings.
