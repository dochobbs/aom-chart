Follow-up and monitoring
Time to response: Expect defervescence and clinical improvement within 24–48 hours of starting antibiotics. Lack of improvement should prompt reassessment for resistant organism, renal abscess, or urinary obstruction.
Recurrence surveillance: Counsel parents on signs of recurrent UTI (fever without source, dysuria, foul-smelling urine). Children with a first febrile UTI have a 10–30% risk of recurrence within the first year.
Antibiotic duration: Current evidence supports 7–10 days for febrile UTI. Recent trials suggest that individualized shorter durations (4–5 days) may be non-inferior for selected low-risk children, though this is not yet standard practice; use the full 7–10 day course unless participating in a structured short-course protocol

Red flags requiring urgent reassessment

Fever persisting beyond 48 hours of antibiotics, inability to tolerate oral fluids, decreased urine output, vomiting, lethargy, flank pain, or new-onset irritability should prompt immediate ED evaluation for possible pyelonephritis with complications (renal abscess, bacteremia) or urosepsis.

What to avoid
Do not use nitrofurantoin for this febrile UTI presentation; it does not achieve adequate tissue levels for pyelonephritis.
Do not obtain VCUG routinely after a single febrile UTI without abnormal imaging or atypical features

Prevention and family counseling
Ensure completion of the full antibiotic course.
Teach parents to monitor for recurrence: any febrile illness in the next several months warrants prompt urinalysis and culture.
Maintain adequate hydration and regular voiding habits.
For a first UTI, antibiotic prophylaxis is not indicated unless high-grade VUR is subsequently diagnosed.
