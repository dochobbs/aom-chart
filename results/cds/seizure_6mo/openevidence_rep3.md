This is most consistent with a febrile seizure in a well-appearing 6-month-old, but the prolonged and possibly under-timed duration plus the child's young age push this out of the "clearly simple" category and lower the threshold for closer observation and CNS evaluation. She now looks well (alert, tracking, consolable, smiling, soft/flat fontanelle, supple neck, no focal deficits, normal tone), which is the single most reassuring feature and argues strongly against active CNS infection at this moment.

Lumbar puncture / CNS infection assessment. Current AAP-era practice no longer mandates routine LP for well-appearing children 6–12 months with febrile seizures; the 1996 routine-LP recommendation was retired, and LP rates in this age group have fallen dramatically without any rise in missed bacterial meningitis.

[4]

Incomplete Hib and pneumococcal immunization, or uncertain vaccine status — verify her 2- and 4-month vaccines were given.

Pretreatment with antibiotics in the preceding days (can mask meningitis) — none reported here.

Given she is now well-appearing with a reassuring neuro exam and normal fontanelle, immediate LP is not automatically required, but her young age (6 months, at the lower boundary), the prolonged/under-timed seizure, and the ~20-minute postictal drowsiness justify a period of in-clinic or ED observation with a low threshold to perform LP if she does not continue to improve or any red flag emerges.

Workup.

No routine labs, neuroimaging, or EEG are indicated for a well-appearing child if this settles as a simple/self-limited febrile seizure; testing should instead be directed at identifying the fever source.
