Name: Not documented Age / Sex: 24 months / Male Race / Ethnicity: Not documented / Not documented Insurance: Medicaid Language: English A 24-month-old boy is brought to clinic by his mother for ear pain. He has had cold symptoms for 5 days. Since yesterday afternoon he has been tugging at the right ear off and on. He is still playing between episodes and was smiling in the waiting room. Mother gave acetaminophen once overnight; it took the edge off. He is eating a little less than usual but taking fluids well. No vomiting, no drainage from the ear. She thought he felt warm last night. Clinic temperature is 101.7°F (38.7°C). Immunizations up to date. No drug allergies. Weight 12.4 kg. Otherwise healthy. Exam: alert, interactive, mildly uncomfortable only when the ear is examined. HR 118, RR 26, SpO2 99% RA. Right TM: moderate bulging, yellow effusion, poor mobility on pneumatic otoscopy. Left TM normal, no effusion. No mastoid tenderness. Remainder of exam unremarkable. What is your plan?

Clinical Guideline

The 2013 AAP Clinical Practice Guideline recommends that clinicians either prescribe antibiotic therapy or offer observation (watchful waiting) with close follow-up for children ≥2 years of age with unilateral, non-severe AOM. Immediate antibiotic therapy is indicated for children <2 years with bilateral AOM, any child with AOM with otorrhea, or those with severe symptoms (moderate-severe otalgia or temperature ≥39°C).

At exactly 24 months, he sits at the guideline boundary. Both immediate antibiotic therapy and watchful waiting with a safety-net prescription are guideline-concordant options.

Recommended Plan

1. Antibiotic decision — Provide a safety-net (delayed/contingent) prescription today

Given his age (exactly 2 years, higher risk stratum than older children), unilateral AOM with moderate bulging and effusion (true AOM by strict otoscopic criteria), and the practical realities of Medicaid follow-up access, offering a dated prescription with instructions to fill only if no improvement in 48–72 hours is appropriate.
