This presentation is acute otitis media (AOM) of the right ear in a 24-month-old meeting diagnostic criteria (moderate TM bulging, yellow effusion, impaired mobility, acute onset ear pain with fever). The child is older than 24 months with unilateral disease and non-severe symptoms, making observation with close follow-up a guideline-supported option alongside immediate antibiotics.

Assessment and risk stratification

Diagnosis: Acute otitis media, right ear, uncomplicated.

Severity: Non-severe (temperature <39°C [102.2°F], mild to moderate ear pain, well-appearing, playful). Age ≥24 months with unilateral involvement.

Bacterial consideration: The most likely pathogens remain Streptococcus pneumoniae, Haemophilus influenzae, and Moraxella catarrhalis.

Immediate management

Pain control is mandatory regardless of antibiotic strategy.

Acetaminophen: 15 mg/kg/dose orally every 4–6 hours as needed (max 75 mg/kg/day, not to exceed adult maximum). For 12.4 kg: 185 mg per dose (approximately 3 mL of 160 mg/5 mL suspension).
Ibuprofen: 10 mg/kg/dose orally every 6–8 hours as needed (for children ≥6 months). For 12.4 kg: 124 mg per dose (approximately 5 mL of 100 mg/5 mL suspension). Maximum 40 mg/kg/day.
Treatment decision: observation versus antibiotics

For children 24 months or older with unilateral AOM and non-severe symptoms, the American Academy of Pediatrics supports either observation with rescue prescription or immediate antibiotics based on shared decision-making.

Approach	Criteria	Instructions
Observation with rescue	Reliable follow-up, non-severe illness, parental comfort with deferring antibiotics	Provide rescue amoxicillin prescription to start if symptoms worsen or fail to improve within 48–72 hours
Immediate antibiotics	Uncertain follow-up, severe illness, or parental preference for treatment	Start high-dose amoxicillin (see below)

Given this child's reliable follow-up at the clinic and mild-moderate presentation, observation with a rescue antibiotic prescription is reasonable provided the family can return promptly if deterioration occurs.

If antibiotics are started (rescue or immediate)

First-line: Amoxicillin 80–90 mg/kg/day divided twice daily for 10 days (or 5–7 days if aged 2–5 years with non-severe AOM and good response, though 10 days remains standard for this age).

Weight: 12.4 kg
Dose: 500–550 mg orally twice daily (using 400 mg/5 mL suspension: 6.25 mL provides 500 mg; using 250 mg/5 mL suspension: 10 mL provides 500 mg).

Alternative if penicillin allergy (non-anaphylactic): Cefdinir 14 mg/kg/day divided once or twice daily (maximum 600 mg/day) or cefuroxime axetil.

Alternative if severe penicillin allergy (anaphylaxis): Azithromycin or clarithromycin (recognizing higher failure rates for H. influenzae).

Follow-up and rescue criteria

If observing:

Reassess within 48–72 hours if not improving, or sooner if worsening.
Start rescue antibiotics if fever persists, pain increases, or new symptoms develop.
Total observation period should not exceed 72 hours without reassessment.

Red flags requiring immediate reassessment:

Persistent or worsening fever beyond 48–72 hours after starting antibiotics.
Increasing pain, irritability, or lethargy.
Swelling or tenderness over the mastoid process (mastoiditis).
Stiff neck, severe headache, or altered mental status.
Perforation with purulent drainage.
Prevention and counseling
Vaccination: Continue age-appropriate pneumococcal conjugate (PCV) and influenza immunizations.
Risk reduction: Avoid secondhand smoke exposure; consider pacifier use only at nap/bedtime and weaning by 12 months if recurrent infections develop.
Adherence: If antibiotics are started, complete the full course even if symptoms improve.
When to seek care: If ear pain becomes severe, if fluid persists >3 months with hearing concerns, or if language development seems delayed (indicating possible hearing loss from chronic effusion).
Common pitfalls
Anchoring on viral illness: Do not attribute bulging TM with impaired mobility and fever solely to viral URI; this meets AOM diagnostic criteria and warrants treatment decision-making.
Under-treating pain: Failure to provide adequate analgesia is common; pain control improves feeding and sleep regardless of antibiotic strategy.
Antibiotic overuse: Immediate antibiotics are not superior to observation with rescue for older children with non-severe unilateral AOM, and observation reduces antibiotic exposure and resistance selection.
Duration errors: Ensure 10-day course for children <2 years or with severe AOM; 5–7 days may be considered only for children ≥2 years with mild disease and rapid response.

Bottom line: For this 24-month-old with right unilateral AOM, non-severe symptoms, and reliable follow-up, observation with a rescue prescription and strict analgesia is appropriate. If the family prefers immediate treatment or follow-up is uncertain, start high-dose amoxicillin 500 mg orally twice daily for 10 days with acetaminophen and/or ibuprofen for pain.
