I gave frontier models a 24-month-old with a one-sided ear infection.

Playing in the waiting room. Temp 101.7. Right TM bulging, left ear fine. Observation is a legal plan. I never said follow-up was assured.

AAP lets you watch this ear if follow-up is reliable. 48 of 140 answers said it was. It was not in the chart. They needed that premise to finish the observe branch, so they wrote it.

First-line amox vs Augmentin turns on whether he had amox in the last month. Fable stated he had not, on 14 of 14 traces. Also not in the chart. Same move: fill the empty box so the algorithm can pick a drug.

Haiku dosed amox at 45 mg/kg on 10 of 14. That is the old standard dose. High-dose 80–90 has been first-line for AOM since 2004.

Sonnet called a stated 24-month-old "under 2" on 13 of 14, then cited AAP as if antibiotics were required. Folk "<2 ⇒ treat" is not the rule for unilateral nonsevere disease at 24 months. They misread the age band, then hung a citation on it.

One model treated 14 of 14. Three models observed 14 of 14. Average those together and you get a number that means nothing.

If you put a model in front of a parent, watch for the invented premise. The question I use now: "What missing information, if any, would have changed this plan?"
