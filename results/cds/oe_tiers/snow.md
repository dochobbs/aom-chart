NEJM Review

This is a nonsevere, unilateral acute otitis media (AOM) in a 24-month-old, and both a short course of high-dose amoxicillin and watchful waiting are defensible. According to an invited review in the New England Journal of Medicine, the moderately bulging tympanic membrane with a yellow effusion and impaired mobility confirms AOM — bulging of the tympanic membrane is the defining diagnostic feature that distinguishes true AOM from otitis media with effusion. 
NEJM
[1]
 Because symptoms of AOM abate without antibiotics in most children and suppurative complications are very rare, the review states that expectant observation without immediate antibiotics is a reasonable strategy in nonsevere cases, provided reliable follow-up is assured. 
NEJM
[1]

When the decision is to treat, the review endorses high-dose amoxicillin, 80 to 90 mg/kg/day divided into two doses, as first-line therapy. 
NEJM
[1]
 Amoxicillin–clavulanate is reserved for children in whom H. influenzae is likely to predominate — those who received antibiotics within the prior 30 days, have conjunctivitis–otitis syndrome, or have spontaneous tympanic membrane rupture — none of which apply here. 
NEJM
[1]
 Oral cephalosporins are generally discouraged because they are substantially less effective than amoxicillin at eradicating penicillin-nonsusceptible S. pneumoniae. 
NEJM
[1]
 On duration, the review notes that a 10-day course produced less treatment failure and less rescue-antibiotic use than a 5-day course, a finding derived from children 6–23 months of age. 
NEJM
[1]
 Analgesia is emphasized as central to management regardless of the antibiotic decision, with antibiotic benefits (a modest reduction in pain by days 2–3) weighed against harms — diarrhea occurs in roughly 1 in 8 children treated with amoxicillin.

Recommended plan for this specific child

This boy is 24 months old, with unilateral AOM, a temperature of 38.7°C (below the 39°C/102.2°F severe threshold), ear pain of less than 48 hours that is only mild (playing and interactive between episodes, taking fluids well, no otorrhea, no mastoid signs). By AAP/Red Book criteria this is nonsevere, unilateral AOM in a child ≥24 months, which places him squarely in the group eligible for either immediate antibiotics or observation through shared decision-making with his mother.

Preferred approach — shared-decision observation with a safety net. Given nonsevere unilateral disease, good hydration, reassuring appearance, and up-to-date immunizations, watchful waiting for 48–72 hours is well supported; most cases resolve spontaneously and antibiotics add only modest symptomatic benefit while carrying a ~1-in-8 risk of diarrhea. Provide a "safety-net" (delayed) prescription to be filled only if he is not improving or worsens within 48–72 hours, or arrange a reassessment visit in that window.
