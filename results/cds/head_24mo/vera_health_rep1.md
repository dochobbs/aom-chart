A 24-month-old boy is brought to clinic by his father after a fall at home.

About 2 hours ago he fell off the living-room couch onto a hardwood floor. Father was in the kitchen and heard the thud; the boy was crying when he got to him. He vomited once in the car on the way in. No seizure activity. Immunizations up to date. No drug allergies. Weight 12.8 kg. Otherwise healthy.

Exam: alert, walks to the toy bin and plays, GCS 15. HR 112, RR 24, SpO2 99% RA. 3 cm soft boggy swelling over the occiput, no step-off, no laceration. Pupils equal and reactive. No hemotympanum, no Battle sign, no periorbital bruising. Fontanelle closed. No bruising elsewhere on full skin exam. Neuro non-focal, gait normal. Remainder of exam unremarkable.

What is your plan?

Searched evidence

Transfer to an emergency department for structured observation with immediate access to pediatric neuroimaging; do not obtain routine immediate CT solely for this presentation.

CLINICAL GUIDELINE

For children with minor head injury and intermediate-risk features, use age-appropriate PECARN risk stratification and favor structured observation over immediate CT when findings are isolated; isolated vomiting is an uncommon presentation of clinically important traumatic brain injury. 
Emergency Medicine Australasia
2021

Risk assessment

At exactly 24 months, apply the PECARN rule for children aged ≥2 years. He has:
GCS 15, normal behavior and neurologic examination
No suspected palpable/depressed or basilar skull fracture
No seizure or loss of consciousness
One vomiting episode
No clearly severe mechanism, assuming the couch fall was not >5 feet
The occipital swelling should be carefully re-palpated. If it is unusually boggy, enlarging, tender, associated with a palpable defect, or the mechanism is uncertain, lower the threshold for CT.

ED plan

Perform serial assessments of mental status/behavior, pupils, gait, vomiting, headache or irritability, and scalp swelling; reassess hydration and oral intake. The exact observation duration is institution-dependent, but observing until approximately 6 hours after injury is reasonable in this otherwise well child.
Immediate noncontrast head CT if there is deterioration, recurrent/persistent vomiting, abnormal behavior or somnolence, worsening headache, seizure, focal deficit, GCS decline, or concern for skull fracture. CT is also reasonable if reliable observation or follow-up cannot be ensured.
Do not use skull radiographs as a screening test; they do not exclude clinically important intracranial injury.

If he remains at baseline, has no further vomiting, tolerates fluids, and the father can provide reliable observation and return promptly, discharge without CT is reasonable. Provide explicit return precautions for repeated vomiting, increasing drowsiness, confusion or unusual behavior, seizure, weakness, gait abnormality, worsening headache, or clear fluid from the nose or ears. Reassess promptly if any concern for nonaccidental trauma emerges.
