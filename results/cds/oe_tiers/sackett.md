This child is non-severe: temperature 38.7°C is below the 39°C threshold, otalgia began <48 hours ago, he is playing and interactive with only mild discomfort, and there is no otorrhea. Under the AAP, immediate antibiotics are mandatory only for children <6 months, any child with otorrhea or severe signs (temp ≥39°C, otalgia ≥48 h, or moderate-to-severe pain), and children 6–23 months with bilateral AOM. Everyone else in the 6–23 month unilateral non-severe group and all children ≥24 months without severe symptoms qualifies for observation with close follow-up.

Analgesia is the mainstay either way and should be optimized now regardless of the antibiotic decision — both oral acetaminophen and ibuprofen effectively relieve otalgia. 
Nature Reviews

 At 12.4 kg, weight-based dosing is roughly:

Acetaminophen 15 mg/kg ≈ 185 mg (≈ 5 mL of 160 mg/5 mL) every 4–6 h as needed.

Ibuprofen (appropriate at >6 months) 10 mg/kg ≈ 125 mg (≈ 6 mL of 100 mg/5 mL) every 6–8 h as needed with food.

Two reasonable paths:

Observation (watchful waiting) with a rescue/delayed prescription. Reassess in 48–72 hours (return visit or a prescription to fill only if he worsens or fails to improve). This is well supported: antibiotics offer only modest benefit — NNT ~20 for pain at 2–3 days — most cases resolve spontaneously, and ~1 in 14 treated children has an adverse event (diarrhea, rash) that would not otherwise occur. Studies suggest only ~3–6% of children managed this way ultimately need the antibiotic.

Immediate high-dose amoxicillin, the first-line agent when antibiotics are chosen (no amoxicillin in the prior 30 days, no purulent conjunctivitis, no penicillin allergy). Dose 80–90 mg/kg/day divided BID; for 12.4 kg this is ≈ 500 mg (6.25 mL of 400 mg/5 mL) twice daily. Amoxicillin has lower failure/recurrence rates than amoxicillin-clavulanate, cefdinir, or azithromycin in comparative data.
