What is your plan?

Searched evidence

This is most consistent with a first generalized febrile seizure, likely “simple” if the total seizure duration was <15 minutes. Because the timing began after onset, she is only 6 months old, and this was her first event, I would arrange same-day pediatric emergency-department evaluation and observation, rather than discharge directly from clinic.

Immediate plan
Reassess airway, breathing, circulation, temperature, hydration, and neurologic status; document the seizure semiology and obtain the father’s video/timing information if available.
Check point-of-care glucose if mental status, intake, or clinical condition becomes abnormal; it is not routinely required in a fully recovered, well-appearing child with a typical simple febrile seizure.
Evaluate the fever source. The upper-respiratory findings support a viral illness, but obtain urinalysis/urine culture if the source is not convincing, fever persists, or urinary infection is clinically plausible.
Testing generally not indicated now

If she remains alert with a normal neurologic examination and no signs of central nervous system infection, do not routinely obtain complete blood count, electrolytes, electroencephalography, computed tomography, magnetic resonance imaging, or lumbar puncture. Routine testing is not recommended after a typical simple febrile seizure, and emergent neuroimaging is not recommended even for a complex febrile seizure when the child returns to baseline without neurologic deficits.
