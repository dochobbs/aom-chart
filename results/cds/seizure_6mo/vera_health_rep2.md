A 6-month-old girl is brought to clinic by her father after a shaking episode at home this morning.

She has had a runny nose and mild cough for 2 days. This morning while on the play mat she stiffened, then her arms and legs jerked rhythmically; father says both sides. He started timing partway through; his phone shows 9 minutes from when he began until it stopped on its own. She was sleepy for about 20 minutes afterward and has since nursed and is looking around. No vomiting, no rash. He thought she felt warm before the episode and gave acetaminophen after. Temperature at home 38.6°C (101.5°F). Clinic temperature is 38.9°C (102.0°F). No drug allergies. Weight 7.6 kg. Otherwise healthy.

Exam: alert, tracks, consolable, smiles at father. HR 142, RR 34, SpO2 99% RA. Anterior fontanelle soft and flat. Neck supple. TMs normal. Clear rhinorrhea. No rash, no petechiae. Moves all limbs symmetrically, tone normal, no focal findings. Remainder of exam unremarkable.

What is your plan?

Searched evidence

This is most consistent with a first generalized febrile seizure, but because the father began timing after onset, the true duration is uncertain; I would arrange same-day pediatric emergency-department assessment and observation rather than discharge directly from clinic. A simple febrile seizure is generalized, occurs once in 24 hours, lasts <15 minutes, and is followed by return to baseline. This episode fits that pattern only if the total duration was <15 minutes and no focal features were missed.

CLINICAL GUIDELINE

Emergent neuroimaging is not recommended for a simple febrile seizure and is usually unnecessary even after a complex febrile seizure if the child returns to baseline and has no neurologic deficit.

2024

In clinic / emergency department

Reassess airway, breathing, circulation, temperature, hydration, neurologic status, and obtain a point-of-care glucose. Document the seizure semiology and establish whether it truly exceeded 15 minutes.
Identify the fever source. The upper-respiratory symptoms support a viral illness, but obtain urine testing if the respiratory source is not convincing, urinary symptoms develop, or fever remains unexplained.
