Analyzed query, searched for evidence

Observation in clinic with shared decision-making, rather than immediate CT, is the appropriate plan for this child. At 24 months (≥2 years) with GCS 15, a normal and non-focal neurologic exam, no signs of basilar skull fracture, and no altered mental status, he does not meet any PECARN high-risk criterion. His single episode of vomiting places him in the PECARN intermediate-risk group, where the recommendation is observation versus CT based on clinician experience, caregiver preference, and whether findings are isolated or multiple — not mandatory imaging.

Why he is intermediate (not high) risk

For children ≥2 years, high-risk features mandating CT are GCS 14/altered mental status or signs of basilar skull fracture — none present.

Recommended plan

Structured in-clinic observation for ~4–6 hours from the time of injury, with serial neuro checks (mental status, pupils, activity, gait) and monitoring for repeat vomiting. If symptoms resolve and he continues to play, eat, and behave normally, CT can be safely deferred.
