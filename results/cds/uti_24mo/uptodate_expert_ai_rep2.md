Antibiotic choice and duration

Preferred empiric outpatient therapy is a first-generation cephalosporin, with cephalexin preferred when local resistance to first-generation cephalosporins is not ≥15 percent. Treat febrile UTI for 10 days, then tailor to urine culture and susceptibilities.
+1

Follow-up and response expectations

Maintain close communication with caregivers for the first 2 to 3 days, emphasizing seriousness of infection and completing the full course. Clinical condition should improve within 24 to 48 hours; if clinical condition worsens or fails to improve as expected within 48 to 72 hours, hospital admission for parenteral therapy is recommended. Do not routinely repeat urine cultures if the child has expected clinical response and the organism is susceptible to the antibiotic used.
+3

Imaging after this first febrile UTI

Plan kidney and bladder ultrasound after a first febrile UTI (unless a normal prenatal ultrasound at a reputable center after 30 to 32 weeks is available and accessible). Reserve VCUG for recurrence (≥2 febrile UTIs) or for a first febrile UTI with abnormal ultrasound, non–E. coli pathogen, poor growth, or hypertension. Indications for imaging in children with urinary tract infections
+2

Longer-term counseling

Counsel caregivers to seek prompt evaluation for future febrile illnesses, including urinalysis and urine culture, to detect recurrent UTI early. Primary care follow-up should include monitoring of height, weight, and blood pressure.
