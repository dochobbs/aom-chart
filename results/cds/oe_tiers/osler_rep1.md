NEJM Review

A 24-month-old with nonsevere, unilateral acute otitis media can reasonably be managed either with immediate high-dose amoxicillin or with close observation, and pain control is essential either way. According to an invited review in the New England Journal of Medicine, the diagnosis of acute otitis media rests on tympanic-membrane bulging, which this child demonstrates (moderate bulging, yellow effusion, poor mobility on pneumatic otoscopy) — distinguishing true acute otitis media from otitis media with effusion, which does not warrant antibiotics. 
NEJM
[1]

The review notes that because symptoms of acute otitis media abate rapidly without antibiotics and suppurative complications are rare, expectant observation without immediate antibiotics is a reasonable strategy in nonsevere cases. 
NEJM
[1]
 A meta-analysis of 13 randomized trials found antibiotics produced a 29% lower risk of persistent symptoms at 2–3 days (number needed to treat 20), a 24% lower risk at 4–7 days (NNT 17), and a 67% lower risk at 10–12 days (NNT 7); antibiotics also halved contralateral episodes (NNT 11) and reduced perforations (NNT 33). 
NEJM
[1]
 These benefits must be weighed against harms — diarrhea occurred in roughly 1 in 8 children treated with amoxicillin (number needed to harm 15) and 1 in 5 treated with amoxicillin–clavulanate. 
NEJM
[1]

When antibiotics are chosen, the review endorses high-dose amoxicillin (80–90 mg/kg/day divided into two doses) as first-line therapy. 
NEJM
[1]
 Amoxicillin–clavulanate is reserved for children in whom H. influenzae is likely to predominate — those who received antibiotics within the prior 30 days, have conjunctivitis–otitis syndrome, or have spontaneous tympanic-membrane rupture — none of which apply here. 
NEJM
[1]
 Oral cephalosporins are generally discouraged because they are substantially less effective than amoxicillin at eradicating penicillin-nonsusceptible S. pneumoniae. 
NEJM
[1]
 On duration, the review notes a 10-day course resulted in less treatment failure and less rescue-antibiotic use than a 5-day course.

Recommended plan:

Analgesia is the priority regardless of the antibiotic decision. Weight-based acetaminophen (10–15 mg/kg every 4–6 hours; ~125–185 mg per dose at 12.4 kg) and/or ibuprofen (10 mg/kg every 6–8 hours; ~125 mg per dose) should be scheduled around the clock for the first 24–48 hours, since pain is the dominant symptom and it is undertreated when only used as needed.
