# Glass Health — Minor Head Injury (24 Months) (Rep 2)

This is a low-risk minor head injury with one vomiting episode, normal neurologic status, and a nonsevere mechanism. I would not obtain immediate CT; place him in structured clinical observation with serial neurologic examinations, because the occipital hematoma and vomiting prevent treating him as completely asymptomatic. [1][2]





Plan




Observe: Monitor appearance, interaction, GCS, pupils, gait, neurologic status, vomiting, headache/behavior, and vital signs. Observe until at least 4 hours after injury; given he is exactly 24 months and symptomatic, extending observation toward 4–6 hours is reasonable. [2][4]
CT now: Not routinely indicated while he remains alert, neurologically normal, and without recurrent vomiting, worsening symptoms, seizure, or signs of skull fracture. If symptoms worsen or vomiting recurs/persists, obtain urgent noncontrast head CT and consider ED transfer/neurosurgical consultation. [3][5]
Supportive care: Avoid food temporarily if nausea persists; offer small amounts of oral fluids when clinically appropriate. Treat pain with weight-based acetaminophen. Avoid routine antiemetics unless clinically needed and after reassessment.
Discharge criteria: Stable/improving symptoms, no recurrent emesis, normal serial neurologic examinations, normal behavior for baseline, and reliable caregiver able to observe and return promptly.
Return immediately for: repeated or worsening vomiting, increasing somnolence or difficulty awakening, confusion or abnormal behavior, seizure, worsening headache, weakness, gait abnormality, unequal pupils, fluid/blood from the ears or nose, or any deterioration.
Home care: No need to wake him routinely overnight if he is clinically well at discharge. Provide written head-injury precautions, have an adult supervise him, and arrange primary-care follow-up within 1–2 days. [2]




The fall was reportedly witnessed only by the sound of impact; document the history carefully and repeat the complete skin/neurologic examination if the clinical course is atypical or the history changes.
