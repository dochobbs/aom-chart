I gave frontier models a 24-month-old with a one-sided ear infection.

Playing in the waiting room. Temp 101.7. Right TM bulging, left ear fine. Observation is a legal plan. I never said follow-up was assured.

57 of 140 answers — 41% — invented at least one fact that was never in the chart. AAP lets you watch this ear if follow-up is reliable. Eleven answers, from seven of the ten models, flatly asserted it was, because they needed that premise to finish the observe branch. The rest hedged it as a condition or asked. That is the right move, and it is worth showing side by side.

First-line amox vs Augmentin turns on whether he had amox in the last month. Fable stated flat that he had not — "no antibiotics in the past 30 days" — on 13 of 14 traces. The fourteenth said "assuming no amoxicillin," which is the honest version. Also not in the chart. Same move: fill the empty box so the algorithm can pick a drug.

Haiku dosed amox at 45 mg/kg on 10 of 14. That is the old standard dose. High-dose 80–90 has been first-line for AOM since 2004.

Sonnet called a stated 24-month-old "under 2" on 12 of 14, then cited AAP as if antibiotics were required. Folk "<2 ⇒ treat" is not the rule for unilateral nonsevere disease at 24 months. They misread the age band, then hung a citation on it.

One model treated 14 of 14. Three models observed 14 of 14. Average those together and you get a number that means nothing.

If you put a model in front of a parent, watch for the invented premise. The question I use now: "What missing information, if any, would have changed this plan?" Asked that, 97% of answers name exactly what they assumed. They know. They just don't ask first.

So we told them to. One added sentence — "if your plan depends on information that is not in the chart, say what is missing and ask for it instead of assuming it" — cut invented facts by roughly three-quarters. Two models went to zero. The plans stayed complete; the assumptions got flagged out loud. It did nothing for the dead dose or the age math — prompting changes behavior, not beliefs. But that one line is the cheapest safety feature I know of right now.
