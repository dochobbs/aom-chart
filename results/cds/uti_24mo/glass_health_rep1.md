# Glass Health — First Febrile UTI (24 Months) (Rep 1)

This is a first febrile UTI, clinically consistent with upper-tract involvement despite the absence of flank findings. He is well appearing, hydrated, tolerating oral intake, and without sepsis features, so outpatient oral therapy is appropriate pending culture. The catheterized UA meets criteria for pyuria and bacteriuria; confirm with growth of a single organism, generally ≥50,000 CFU/mL in a catheterized specimen. [1][5]





Treatment




Start empiric oral antibiotic today, selected according to the local pediatric antibiogram and prior culture history if available. Reasonable choices commonly include cephalexin or cefixime; avoid empiric therapy with agents having substantial local resistance.
Treat for 7–10 days as a febrile UTI/possible pyelonephritis rather than a short cystitis course. Adjust or narrow therapy promptly when culture and susceptibilities return. [2][3]
Dose by the current weight of 12.6 kg and the selected formulation; verify renal function, recent antibiotic exposure, and local susceptibility before specifying the prescription.
Antipyresis may be provided for discomfort, with continued fluids and normal feeding as tolerated.




Follow-up and safety net




Review the culture and susceptibility results within 48–72 hours and contact the family if the organism is resistant, the culture is negative/contaminated, or treatment needs modification.
Expect clinical improvement, including defervescence, within approximately 48–72 hours. Reassess sooner for worsening appearance, poor intake, reduced urine output, persistent vomiting, increasing pain, or respiratory/sepsis concerns; lack of improvement by 48–72 hours warrants repeat examination and reconsideration of diagnosis, resistance, complications, or imaging. [3]
No routine repeat urine culture is needed if he improves clinically. Evaluate urine during future unexplained febrile illnesses rather than using scheduled surveillance cultures. [4]




Imaging




Arrange a renal and bladder ultrasound after this first febrile UTI, ensuring that the bladder and kidneys are assessed; timing should follow local pediatric radiology workflow. [1]
Do not obtain VCUG routinely after a first febrile UTI. Consider VCUG or pediatric urology/nephrology referral if ultrasound is abnormal, the infection is atypical or non–E. coli, the course is poor, or febrile UTIs recur. [1][5]
