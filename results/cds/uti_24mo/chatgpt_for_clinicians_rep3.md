A 24-month-old boy is brought to clinic by his mother for fever.

He has been fussy and warm for 2 days. Mother has not measured a temperature at home. He is drinking well but eating less. One loose stool yesterday. No vomiting, no cough, no runny nose, no rash. Wet diapers as usual. Clinic temperature is 38.4°C (101.1°F). Immunizations up to date. No drug allergies. Weight 12.6 kg. Otherwise healthy.

Exam: alert, fussy but consolable, drinking from a cup in the room. HR 128, RR 28, SpO2 99% RA. TMs normal. Throat clear. Lungs clear. Abdomen soft, non-tender, no masses. No CVA tenderness. Genital exam unremarkable. No rash. Remainder of exam unremarkable.

Catheterized urinalysis: leukocyte esterase 2+, nitrite positive, 30 WBC/hpf, many bacteria. Urine culture sent.

What is your plan?

Assessment

This is a febrile UTI, clinically managed as an upper UTI/pyelonephritis in a well-appearing 24-month-old. Fever ≥38°C plus bacteriuria supports upper UTI in this age group; the catheterized UA is strongly supportive, although definitive microbiologic confirmation depends on the culture. 
ncbi.nlm.nih.gov
+1

Management

Treat as an outpatient given his reassuring appearance, normal perfusion/oxygenation, good oral intake, and absence of vomiting or sepsis features. Oral therapy is appropriate for nontoxic children with febrile UTI. 
Pediatric Nephrology
+1

Start empiric oral antibiotic today; do not wait for the culture. Selection should reflect the local antibiogram and previous isolates/exposures. A reasonable option where local susceptibility supports it is cefixime 8 mg/kg/day PO, given once daily or divided q12h, generally for 7–10 days for febrile/upper UTI.

Avoid nitrofurantoin for this febrile presentation because it does not provide appropriate renal parenchymal treatment for upper UTI. 
AAP

Review urine culture and susceptibilities at 24–48 hours and narrow/change therapy accordingly. A catheterized specimen with pyuria plus significant growth of a single uropathogen confirms UTI; antimicrobial choice should then follow susceptibility results. 
PEDIATRICS
+1

Renal/bladder ultrasound (RBUS) after this first febrile UTI is reasonable and remains supported by current pediatric urology guidance; it need not be emergent in a child improving normally. 
Journal of Pediatric Urology
 Early imaging during the acute inflammatory period can produce transient abnormalities, so outpatient imaging after clinical improvement is reasonable. 
Hospital Pediatrics

Do not routinely obtain VCUG after the first uncomplicated febrile UTI. Consider it for abnormal RBUS, recurrent febrile UTI, atypical/complex infection, or other concerning features. 
AAP
+1
 Routine prophylactic antibiotics are also not indicated after a single uncomplicated UTI. 
ncbi.nlm.nih.gov

Monitoring

Reassess if fever or clinical condition is not clearly improving within 24–48 hours; confirm culture susceptibility and consider resistant organism, obstruction, renal/perinephric complication, or alternative diagnosis. 
ncbi.nlm.nih.gov
 Seek urgent evaluation for toxic appearance, inability to maintain hydration, persistent vomiting, decreased urine output, lethargy, or other signs of sepsis.
