Immediate steps in clinic

Reassess severity to confirm no hospitalization triggers are present, focusing on overall appearance, degree of respiratory distress, and ability to maintain hydration. Key admission triggers to actively rule out include SpO₂ persistently <90% on room air, moderate to severe respiratory distress (including RR >50/min for children ≥12 months), dehydration, toxic appearance, complications, or inability to provide care at home.

Testing

If the child is well enough for outpatient treatment, routine labs and chest radiograph are generally not required.

Antibiotic choice

For most children ≥5 years with CAP, empiric outpatient therapy is typically a macrolide (often azithromycin), but amoxicillin is preferred when the presentation strongly suggests typical bacterial pneumonia. Given focal exam findings and fever, empiric therapy targeting typical bacterial CAP with amoxicillin is reasonable.

Follow-up and expected course

Arrange follow-up within 24 to 48 hours (visit or phone). Clinical improvement is generally expected within 48 to 72 hours with appropriate management.
