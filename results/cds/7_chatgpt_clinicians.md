Assessment

This is definite right acute otitis media, based on moderate TM bulging plus middle-ear effusion and reduced mobility. He has nonsevere unilateral AOM: otalgia <48 hours, temperature 38.7°C (<39°C), no otorrhea, and he is well appearing. 
CDC

Management

Observation/watchful waiting is my preferred initial approach, assuming the family can reliably follow up. At exactly 24 months, he falls into the AAP ≥24-month group, in whom unilateral or bilateral nonsevere AOM can be managed with either observation or antibiotics through shared decision-making. Current CDC stewardship guidance likewise supports observation for mild AOM in children ≥2 years. 
CDC

Treat the pain aggressively. Use weight-appropriate acetaminophen or, since he is >6 months, ibuprofen as needed for otalgia/fever. Analgesia is important regardless of whether antibiotics are used. 
Pediatrics in Review

Set up a 48–72-hour safety net. I would either provide a delayed prescription or arrange easy phone/clinic follow-up. Start antibiotics sooner if pain/fever clearly worsens; otherwise start if he is not improving within 48–72 hours. 
CDC

If antibiotics are needed: first-line is amoxicillin 80–90 mg/kg/day PO divided BID, assuming no amoxicillin in the previous 30 days, no purulent conjunctivitis, and no penicillin allergy. For a 2-year-old with mild/moderate AOM, a 7-day course is appropriate.
I would not independently convert that to an exact mg or mL dose here without a validated pediatric dosing calculator.

Escalate/re-examine for new postauricular swelling/tenderness, protrusion of the auricle, toxic appearance, neurologic symptoms, persistent significant fever, otorrhea, or failure to improve after 48–72 hours of appropriate antibiotics. Antibiotic treatment failure warrants reassessment and potentially broader β-lactamase coverage.

So, for this child: analgesia + observation with a delayed amoxicillin prescription and guaranteed 48–72-hour follow-up is a guideline-concordant plan.
