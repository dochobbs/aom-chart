Analyzed query, searched for evidence

Outpatient oral antibiotics with close follow-up is appropriate here — this is a febrile UTI (likely pyelonephritis given fever without another source) in a well-appearing, well-hydrated 24-month-old who is drinking readily. The catheterized specimen showing 2+ leukocyte esterase, positive nitrite, 30 WBC/hpf, and many bacteria supports both pyuria and probable bacteriuria, satisfying the AAP criteria for presumptive UTI pending culture confirmation. Children older than 2 months who tolerate oral intake and do not appear toxic can be managed entirely with oral antibiotics — parenteral therapy and hospitalization are reserved for ill-appearing, dehydrated, or non-retaining children, none of which apply here.

Antibiotic selection — guided by your local antibiogram

Start empiric oral therapy promptly (within 48 hours of fever onset reduces the risk of renal scarring). Reasonable first-line options for a febrile UTI/presumed pyelonephritis include an oral third-generation cephalosporin (e.g., cefdinir, cefixime, or cefpodoxime), cephalexin, or amoxicillin-clavulanate, chosen according to local resistance patterns.

Narrow therapy once culture and sensitivities return.

Duration

7 to 10 days is standard for a febrile UTI/presumed pyelonephritis with good clinical response per AAP-aligned guidance. Some sources support 7 days for uncomplicated febrile UTI, and recent randomized data (5 days of amoxicillin-clavulanate) suggest shorter courses may be adequate in well-appearing children, though this is not yet standard practice.

Supportive care and caregiver counseling

Antipyretics (acetaminophen ~15 mg/kg/dose) for fever and irritability; encourage continued oral hydration.

Return precautions: persistent fever beyond 48–72 hours, worsening appearance, poor oral intake, decreased urine output, or vomiting that prevents keeping medication down — any of these warrants reevaluation and possible parenteral therapy.

Imaging

Obtain a kidney and bladder ultrasound (RBUS) after this first febrile UTI, as recommended for all children under 24 months. It can be deferred until after the acute illness resolves to avoid false-positive inflammatory findings, unless the course is unusually severe or fever persists >48–72 hours.
