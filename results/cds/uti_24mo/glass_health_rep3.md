# Glass Health — First Febrile UTI (24 Months) (Rep 3)

This is a first febrile UTI, likely upper-tract infection, in a well-appearing 24-month-old who is appropriate for outpatient oral therapy pending culture. The catheterized specimen, nitrite positivity, pyuria, and bacteriuria support treatment rather than observation.





Start empiric oral antibiotic today: cephalexin 25–50 mg/kg/day PO divided 3–4 times daily, using the local antibiogram and renal function history; for 12.6 kg, this is 315–630 mg/day. Given fever, use a 7–10-day total course, then narrow or change based on culture and susceptibility. Confirm the liquid concentration before prescribing. [1][3]
If local resistance or prior culture history makes cephalexin unsuitable, select another oral agent based on local susceptibility data. Avoid nitrofurantoin for suspected febrile upper-tract infection.
Follow the culture closely: contact the family when finalized, generally within 48–72 hours; adjust therapy to the organism and susceptibilities. Reassess sooner if the child worsens or is not clearly improving within 48 hours.
Supportive care: encourage fluids; use acetaminophen 15 mg/kg PO every 4–6 hours as needed for discomfort or fever. For 12.6 kg, dose is approximately 190 mg per dose. Ibuprofen may be used if age-appropriate and hydration remains adequate.
Return urgently for lethargy, toxic appearance, persistent vomiting, inability to maintain hydration or medications, reduced urine output, respiratory difficulty, worsening pain, or clinical deterioration.
Imaging: do not obtain routine VCUG after a first uncomplicated febrile UTI. Arrange a renal/bladder ultrasound if the course is atypical, the organism is non-E. coli, response is poor, there is abnormal urine flow, recurrent febrile UTI, or other concern for urinary tract abnormality. [2]
Arrange clinical follow-up within 24–48 hours if symptoms are not clearly improving, and document the culture result and treatment response.
