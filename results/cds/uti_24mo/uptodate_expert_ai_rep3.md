This presentation should be managed as a febrile UTI, with immediate empiric antibiotics after urine culture is sent, and outpatient treatment is appropriate if the child remains well-appearing, can take oral meds, and follow-up is reliable.
+2

Antibiotics now, then tailor to culture

For outpatient empiric therapy, a first-generation cephalosporin is suggested as first-line (preferred over third-generation cephalosporins, amoxicillin, or trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole) when local E. coli resistance to first-generation cephalosporins is not high. Definitive therapy should be based on urine culture and susceptibilities.
+1

Disposition and reassessment window

Hospitalization is generally reserved for children with clinical urosepsis, immunocompromise with fever, vomiting or inability to tolerate oral medication, lack of reliable outpatient follow-up, or failure to respond to outpatient therapy. Clinical improvement is expected within 24 to 48 hours; lack of improvement by 48 to 72 hours should prompt admission for parenteral therapy.
+1

Imaging after a first febrile UTI

Kidney and bladder ultrasound is suggested after a first febrile UTI (with the caveat that it may be deferred if a normal prenatal ultrasound was done at a reputable center and results are accessible). VCUG is generally reserved for children with ≥2 febrile UTIs, or after a first febrile UTI if ultrasound is abnormal, the pathogen is not E. coli, or there is poor growth or hypertension.
+2

Follow-up counseling

Caregivers should be instructed to seek prompt evaluation for future febrile illnesses, and follow-up should include monitoring of growth and blood pressure.
