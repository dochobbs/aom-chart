Name: Not documented Age / Sex: 24 months / Male Race / Ethnicity: Not documented / Not documented Insurance: Private (Blue Cross Blue Shield) Language: English A 24-month-old boy is brought to clinic by his mother for ear pain. He has had cold symptoms for 5 days. Since yesterday afternoon he has been tugging at the right ear off and on. He is still playing between episodes and was smiling in the waiting room. Mother gave acetaminophen once overnight; it took the edge off. He is eating a little less than usual but taking fluids well. No vomiting, no drainage from the ear. She thought he felt warm last night. Clinic temperature is 101.7°F (38.7°C). Immunizations up to date. No drug allergies. Weight 12.4 kg. Otherwise healthy. Exam: alert, interactive, mildly uncomfortable only when the ear is examined. HR 118, RR 26, SpO2 99% RA. Right TM: moderate bulging, yellow effusion, poor mobility on pneumatic otoscopy. Left TM normal, no effusion. No mastoid tenderness. Remainder of exam unremarkable. What is your plan?

Treat with high‑dose amoxicillin and optimized analgesia (rather than watchful waiting), because this 24‑month‑old has definite AOM (bulging TM + effusion) and is right at the <2 vs ≥2 years threshold with fever and moderate bulging. 
1

Clinical Guideline

Use high‑dose amoxicillin as first‑line therapy for uncomplicated acute otitis media when antibiotics are indicated; consider observation only for selected nonsevere cases with reliable follow‑up, and treat for 10 days in children <2 years (shorter courses acceptable in older children depending on severity). 
1

1) Antibiotics (first line)

Amoxicillin 80–90 mg/kg/day PO divided BID or TID. 
1

Dose for 12.4 kg: ~1000–1120 mg/day total (e.g., 500 mg PO BID ≈ 81 mg/kg/day).

Duration: 10 days (conservative given borderline age and clear otoscopic findings; also aligned with <2-year guidance in major pediatric guidance). 
2

When to broaden/escalate (plan now):

If no clinical improvement in 48–72 hours (persistent fever/otalgia, worsening appearance), switch to amoxicillin–clavulanate (beta‑lactamase coverage). 
1

2) Pain/fever control (most important symptom intervention)

Schedule/PRN oral analgesics for the first 24–48 hours (pain drives distress; antibiotics do not give immediate relief).

Drug Information

Acetaminophen: 10–15 mg/kg/dose PO q4–6h PRN (max 75 mg/kg/day, max 5 doses/24h). For 12.4 kg: 125–186 mg/dose. 
Acetaminophen; Other Pain Relievers

3) Follow-up / safety net

Reassess (phone or visit) at 48–72 hours or sooner if worsening; this “early failure check” is the key safety element whether treating or observing. 
1

Immediate evaluation if mastoid tenderness/swelling, protruding ear, severe headache, neck stiffness, persistent vomiting, toxic appearance, or new otorrhea.

4) Why this plan is evidence-based for this child (brief)

In rigorously diagnosed AOM in 6–23 month olds, antibiotics (amox‑clav in that trial) reduced clinical failure vs placebo (16% vs 51% by day 10–12, p<0.001) but with more diarrhea/dermatitis—supporting that antibiotics benefit young children with definite AOM, while still emphasizing pain control and stewardship.
