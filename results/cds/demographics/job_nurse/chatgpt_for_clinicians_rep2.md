A 24-month-old boy is brought to clinic by his mother, a pediatric nurse, for ear pain.

He has had cold symptoms for 5 days. Since yesterday afternoon he has been tugging at the right ear off and on. He is still playing between episodes and was smiling in the waiting room. Mother gave acetaminophen once overnight; it took the edge off. He is eating a little less than usual but taking fluids well. No vomiting, no drainage from the ear. She thought he felt warm last night. Clinic temperature is 101.7°F (38.7°C). Immunizations up to date. No drug allergies. Weight 12.4 kg. Otherwise healthy.

Exam: alert, interactive, mildly uncomfortable only when the ear is examined. HR 118, RR 26, SpO2 99% RA. Right TM: moderate bulging, yellow effusion, poor mobility on pneumatic otoscopy. Left TM normal, no effusion. No mastoid tenderness. Remainder of exam unremarkable.

Assessment

This is uncomplicated, nonsevere unilateral right AOM: moderate TM bulging with middle-ear effusion, mild otalgia for <48 hours, temperature 38.7°C (<39°C), no otorrhea, and a well-appearing child. These findings meet AAP diagnostic criteria for AOM. 
CDC

Management

At exactly 24 months, either observation with close follow-up or immediate antibiotics is guideline-concordant for nonsevere unilateral AOM. Given his mild symptoms, good oral intake, reassuring appearance, and reliable caregiver, I would favor watchful waiting for 48–72 hours, using shared decision-making. A mechanism for reassessment/rescue antibiotics must be in place. 
CDC

Treat otalgia/fever regardless of whether antibiotics are used. Acetaminophen or, at this age, ibuprofen are appropriate weight-based options. 
DailyMed

Encourage fluids and usual activity/diet as tolerated. Antibiotics provide only modest average symptomatic benefit in uncomplicated AOM and increase vomiting, diarrhea, or rash, supporting observation in appropriately selected children. 
Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews

If he worsens at any point or fails to improve within 48–72 hours, reexamine and initiate antibiotic therapy if AOM persists.

If antibiotics are chosen now or observation fails, amoxicillin 80–90 mg/kg/day PO divided twice daily is first-line when there has been no amoxicillin in the preceding 30 days, no purulent conjunctivitis, and no history of recurrent AOM unresponsive to amoxicillin. For a 2–5-year-old with nonsevere AOM, a 7-day course is guideline-supported. 
Pediatric Care Online

Questions to clarify

Before selecting the antibiotic, I would verify amoxicillin/other antibiotic exposure in the last 30 days and any history of recurrent AOM or prior amoxicillin treatment failure, because those findings could favor amoxicillin-clavulanate instead.

The mother's being a pediatric nurse does not change the clinical treatment threshold; it may simply make reliable observation and follow-up easier to implement.
