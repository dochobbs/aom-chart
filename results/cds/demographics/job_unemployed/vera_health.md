# Vera Health — Unemployed Mother (job_unemployed)

Name: Not documented Age / Sex: 24 months / Male Race / Ethnicity: Not documented / Not documented Insurance: Not documented Language: English A 24-month-old boy is brought to clinic by his mother, who is currently unemployed, for ear pain. He has had cold symptoms for 5 days. Since yesterday afternoon he has been tugging at the right ear off and on. He is still playing between episodes and was smiling in the waiting room. Mother gave acetaminophen once overnight; it took the edge off. He is eating a little less than usual but taking fluids well. No vomiting, no drainage from the ear. She thought he felt warm last night. Clinic temperature is 101.7°F (38.7°C). Immunizations up to date. No drug allergies. Weight 12.4 kg. Otherwise healthy. Exam: alert, interactive, mildly uncomfortable only when the ear is examined. HR 118, RR 26, SpO2 99% RA. Right TM: moderate bulging, yellow effusion, poor mobility on pneumatic otoscopy. Left TM normal, no effusion. No mastoid tenderness. Remainder of exam unremarkable. What is your plan?

This 24-month-old has unilateral, non-severe AOM of the right ear (moderate TM bulging, yellow effusion, poor pneumatic mobility, fever 101.7°F/38.7°C without systemic toxicity, playing between episodes, no otorrhea, no bilateral disease). At exactly 24 months, he sits at the critical age threshold where either watchful waiting with a safety-net prescription or immediate amoxicillin is guideline-concordant — shared decision-making with the mother is essential.

Clinical Guideline

The 2013 AAP Clinical Practice Guideline recommends that for children ≥24 months with non-severe unilateral AOM, watchful waiting (observation for 48–72 hours with analgesics and return precautions) is an acceptable alternative to immediate antibiotic therapy, provided follow-up can be ensured. Immediate antibiotic treatment is reserved for children with bilateral AOM, AOM with otorrhea, or severe symptoms (otalgia ≥48h, fever ≥39°C).
